**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **3.CYB.2** The student will identify the relationship between passwords and security risk.
  - a. Describe how authentication and authorization protect private information.
  - b. Identify multiple authentication methods.
  - c. Discuss the security risk posed by not having a strong password.

## Prep

- Write authentication method cards: password, PIN, fingerprint, face scan, a code sent to a phone, a security key, a pattern drawn on a screen.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Show the library card. Ask, "What does this card prove? What does it let me do? What does it not let me do?"

## Direct Instruction (6 minutes)

- Define authentication: "Proving you are who you say you are." The library checks your card.
- Define authorization: "What you are allowed to do once the system knows who you are." You may borrow books, but you may not go into the staff office.
- Explain protection: authentication keeps strangers out, and authorization limits what each person can reach.
- Introduce the method cards. Group them: something you know (password, PIN), something you have (phone, key), something you are (fingerprint, face).
- Explain that using two methods together is safer than one.
- Discuss weak passwords: easy to guess or already known to attackers, so anyone could be authenticated as you.

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. The student sorts the method cards into the three groups.
2. For three real-life examples (a tablet, a streaming account with child and adult profiles, a school portal), name the authentication and the authorization.
3. Ask, "What could happen if the password were 1234?"
4. Ask, "Why might a bank ask for a password and a code sent to a phone?"

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student explains the difference between authentication and authorization with their own example, names three authentication methods, and describes one risk of a weak password.

Look for:
- The two terms are distinguished correctly.
- At least three methods are named.
- The risk is specific, such as someone reading messages or pretending to be the student.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Use the library card example for both terms throughout.
- Limit the methods to password, fingerprint, and code sent to a phone.

**For students ready for more**
- Explain why a fingerprint cannot be changed if it is copied, and why that matters.
- Design the authentication and authorization for a pretend clubhouse.

## Extensions

- Notice authentication methods used by family members for a day.
- Set up different authorizations in a pretend game: guest, member, leader.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- These two words are long. Pair them with short meanings: "who are you?" and "what may you do?"
- Keep examples to systems the student has seen.
