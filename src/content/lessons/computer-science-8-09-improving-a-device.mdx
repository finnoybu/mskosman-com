**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **8.CSY.1** The student will recommend and design improvements to computing devices based on the needs of various users.
  - a. Analyze existing computing devices for advantages and limitations.
  - b. Recommend and design improvements to computing devices based on user interactions.

## Prep

- Arrange for someone, ideally a person with different needs from the student's, such as a younger child or an older adult, to perform a few tasks on a device while the student observes.
- Prepare an observation sheet: task, what the user did, where they struggled, what they said.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "What is one thing about a device you use that you would change if you were its designer?"

## Direct Instruction (4 minutes)

- Teach analysis of a device: its purpose and users, its advantages, and its limitations, covering hardware such as size, weight, battery, buttons, and durability, and software such as menus, feedback, and accessibility.
- Explain user interaction as evidence: watch real people use it and note where they hesitate, make errors, or work around problems.
- Define a recommendation: a specific change, the user need it addresses, and the tradeoff it brings.
- Show a design sketch with labels.

## Guided Practice (9 minutes)

1. The student analyzes one device and lists its advantages and limitations.
2. Observe the user performing three tasks and complete the observation sheet.
3. Identify the two biggest difficulties.
4. Sketch one improvement and note its tradeoff.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student presents a recommendation: the device, evidence from the observation, a labeled design for the improvement, and the tradeoffs.

Look for:
- The analysis covers hardware and software.
- Recommendations are based on observed interaction.
- The design addresses a specific user need.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Analyze one device and propose one change.
- Use a prepared observation sheet with prompts.

**For students ready for more**
- Observe a second user with different needs and revise the design.
- Build a paper or cardboard prototype and test it.

## Extensions

- Read reviews of a device and sort the comments into advantages and limitations.
- Look at how a device changed across versions.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Observation reveals problems that users would not think to mention.
- Every improvement has a cost. Stating it is part of a recommendation.
