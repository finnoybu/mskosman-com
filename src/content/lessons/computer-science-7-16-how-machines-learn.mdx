**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **7.DA.2** The student will explain the process and application of computational thinking in machine learning.
  - a. Explain how supervised, unsupervised, and/or reinforcement learning methods utilize decomposition, pattern recognition, abstraction, and algorithms to learn from and make decisions.
  - b. Explore neural networks and its role in machine learning and artificial intelligence.

## Prep

- Draw a small network: three input circles, three middle circles, two output circles, with lines between layers.
- Prepare shape cards with features such as number of sides, curved or straight, and size.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "How do you recognize a friend's face in a crowd? Can you list the rules you use?"

## Direct Instruction (6 minutes)

- Review the three learning methods: supervised (labeled examples), unsupervised (finding groups), and reinforcement (rewards for actions).
- Connect each computational thinking practice. Decomposition: data is broken into features, such as edges and colors in an image. Pattern recognition: the system finds regularities across many examples. Abstraction: it keeps the features that matter and ignores noise. Algorithms: a learning algorithm adjusts the model step by step to reduce errors, and the trained model then makes decisions.
- Introduce neural networks: models made of layers of connected units loosely inspired by brain cells. Each connection has a weight. Inputs pass through layers, and the output is a prediction.
- Explain training: the network compares its output with the right answer and adjusts weights slightly, over many examples.

## Guided Practice (7 minutes)

1. Decompose a shape card into features.
2. Walk a card through the paper network: inputs are the features, the middle layer combines them, and the outputs are the classes.
3. Change a weight by making one line count double and see how the output changes.
4. For one learning method, the student names where each of the four practices appears.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student explains how a supervised learning system uses each of the four practices, and describes a neural network and what training changes.

Look for:
- Each practice is linked to a part of machine learning.
- A neural network is described as layers of weighted connections.
- Training is described as adjusting weights from examples.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Focus on supervised learning.
- Use the paper network with two inputs.

**For students ready for more**
- Explain why networks with many layers can find more complex patterns and why they need more data.
- Explore an interactive neural network demonstration with an adult.

## Extensions

- Explain neural networks to a family member using the paper model.
- Find an application that uses neural networks and guess its inputs and outputs.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- A neural network is a mathematical model. The comparison to a brain is loose.
- Aim for the big picture. The mathematics comes much later.
