**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **1.CSY.1** The student will describe how computing components work together to create a computing system.
  - b. Identify common components of computing systems in different types of computing devices.
  - c. Describe how hardware and software work together to form a computing system.

## Prep

- Draw the chart.
- Gather the devices or find pictures of a tablet, a laptop, a desktop computer, and a smartphone.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Review: "What is hardware? What is software? What do they make together?" Then place two different devices or pictures side by side and ask, "How are these alike?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Explain: "Computing devices look different, but most of them have the same kinds of parts. A part of a system is called a component."
- Go through the common components, pointing to each on one device:
  - A **screen** to show things.
  - A **way to put information in**: a keyboard, a mouse, a touchscreen, or a microphone.
  - A **speaker** to make sound.
  - A **place to store** pictures, apps, and files. It is inside, so we cannot see it.
  - **Software** that tells the hardware what to do.
- Show the same component on a second device: "The laptop has a keyboard you press. The tablet has a keyboard that appears on the screen. Both are ways to put information in."
- Fill in the first column of the chart together with marks.

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. For the second device, the student finds each component and marks the chart.
2. Repeat for a third device.
3. Look across the rows. Ask, "Which components does every device have?"
4. Pick one device and ask, "What happens when you tap an app? Which part is hardware and which is software?"

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

Show a device or picture that has not been used yet. The student names its components and tells you one way its hardware and software work together.

Look for:
- The student identifies at least three components shared by different devices.
- The student notices that the same job can be done by parts that look different.
- The explanation includes both hardware and software.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Compare only two devices.
- Use three rows on the chart: screen, way to put information in, speaker.
- Let the student point while you name.

**For students ready for more**
- Add devices that are less obvious, such as a smartwatch or a game console.
- Ask which components a smart speaker is missing and how people use it anyway.

## Extensions

- Make a "device family" poster with drawings of several devices and lines connecting the matching components.
- At a library or store, spot the components on a self-checkout machine.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Pictures work if you do not have several devices. Real devices are better where available.
- Storage is the hardest component for this age because it is hidden. "The place where your photos are kept" is enough.
- This lesson reuses the comparing skill from Lesson 1: finding what a set has in common.
