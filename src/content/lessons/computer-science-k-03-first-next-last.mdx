**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **K.AP.2** The student will plan and implement algorithms that include sequential order.
  - a. Identify tasks that are completed using sequential step-by-step instructions.
  - b. Recall and state thoughts, ideas, and stories in the form of sequential steps.

## Prep

- Draw or print three picture cards showing one routine in three steps (for handwashing: water on, scrub with soap, dry hands).
- Pick a short story the student already knows well.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "What did you do first when you woke up today? What did you do after that?"

Then act out putting on shoes before socks. Let the student laugh and correct you. Ask, "Why does the order matter?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Explain: "Many things we do have steps. We do the steps in order: first, next, last."
- Show the three routine cards out of order. Think aloud while placing them in order.
- Say the routine using the order words: "First, turn on the water. Next, scrub with soap. Last, dry your hands."
- Tell the student, "Step-by-step instructions that are done in order have a special name. They are called an algorithm."
- Name two or three other tasks that use steps in order: brushing teeth, making a sandwich, getting dressed.

## Guided Practice (4 minutes)

1. Ask, "What else do we do in steps?" Together, name three tasks from home.
2. Choose one. The student tells the steps while you hold up one finger for each step.
3. Read or recall a familiar short story. Together, retell it with "first, next, last."
4. Try one step out of order and ask, "Does it still work?"

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (3 minutes)

Ask the student to choose a task they know well and tell you the steps in order, using the words first, next, and last. Then ask them to retell a different story or something that happened today in order.

Look for:
- The student names at least one task that is done in steps.
- The steps are told in an order that would work.
- The student uses order words, with or without a reminder.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Stay with three steps and use the picture cards as a visual anchor.
- Act out each step with real objects as it is said.
- Offer the sentence starters "First...", "Next...", "Last...".

**For students ready for more**
- Stretch to four or five steps.
- Ask what would happen if one step were left out.

## Extensions

- At the next meal or bedtime, the student calls out the steps of the routine as they happen.
- Cut apart a comic strip or photos from a family outing and put them back in order.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- The standard includes retelling thoughts, ideas, and stories as steps, so include both a task and a story in this lesson.
- "Algorithm" is introduced here as a name only. The student does not need to define it yet.
- If the student skips a step, act out exactly what was said so the gap becomes visible.
- The next lesson has the student draw a plan before telling the steps.
