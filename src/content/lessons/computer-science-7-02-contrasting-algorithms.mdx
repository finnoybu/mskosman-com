**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **7.AP.1** The student will apply computational thinking to design programs to accomplish a task as a means of creative expression or scientific exploration.
  - d. Contrast various algorithms to solve reasoning problems when accomplishing a task.

## Prep

- Number 16 cards and lay them face down in order.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Say, "I am thinking of a number from 1 to 100. Find it in as few guesses as you can." Play once, then ask what strategy the student used.

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Define a reasoning problem: one solved by logical steps, such as searching, deducing, or eliminating possibilities.
- Present two search algorithms on the cards. Linear search checks each card in turn. Binary search checks the middle card and discards the half that cannot contain the target, repeating.
- Count the steps for each in the worst case: 16 against about 4.
- Introduce criteria for contrasting: always correct? how many steps as the problem grows? any requirements, such as binary search needing sorted data? how hard to carry out?

## Guided Practice (7 minutes)

1. The student runs both searches for three targets and records the steps.
2. Ask, "What happens to each if the list doubles?"
3. Attempt the coin puzzle two ways: weighing coins in pairs, and weighing in groups of three.
4. Contrast the two using the criteria.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student writes a short contrast of two algorithms for one of the problems: how each works, how they differ in steps, and when each is the better choice.

Look for:
- Both algorithms are described accurately.
- The contrast uses evidence such as step counts.
- Requirements and tradeoffs are noted.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Work with the two search algorithms only.
- Use 8 cards.

**For students ready for more**
- Predict the worst case for 1,000 items for each search.
- Design a third algorithm and place it in the comparison.

## Extensions

- Find the fastest strategy for a guessing game.
- Contrast two strategies for solving a maze.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- The key insight is how the number of steps grows, not the count for one small example.
- Binary search only works on sorted data. A fast algorithm can have requirements.
