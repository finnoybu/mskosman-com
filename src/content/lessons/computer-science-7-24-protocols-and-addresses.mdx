**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **7.NI.1** The student will describe and explain why protocols are essential in data transmission.
  - a. Define packet, router, and protocol.
  - b. Describe the process of sending a file through a network.
  - c. Explain the role of Internet Protocol (IP) addresses in transmitting information.

## Prep

- Set up stations: sender, three routers, and receiver.
- Write made-up addresses for each station, such as four numbers separated by dots.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "How does a letter mailed in Virginia reach one specific house in another country? What rules make that possible?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Define packet: a small unit of data with a header that holds addressing and sequence information, and a payload, which is the data itself.
- Define router: a device that reads a packet's destination address and forwards it toward that destination.
- Define protocol: an agreed set of rules for how data is formatted, addressed, sent, and received. Devices from different makers communicate because they follow the same protocols.
- Explain IP addresses: a unique numeric address for a device on a network. Every packet carries the sender's and receiver's addresses so that routers know where to send it and where replies should go.
- Outline sending a file: split it into packets, add headers, route them, receive, check, and reassemble.

## Guided Practice (8 minutes)

1. Cut the document into six pieces and number them.
2. Put each in an envelope with source address, destination address, and sequence number.
3. Route the envelopes through different routers, each reading only the address.
4. The receiver reassembles the document in order.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student defines packet, router, and protocol, describes the steps of sending a file, and explains what would happen without IP addresses or without shared protocols.

Look for:
- The three terms are defined accurately.
- The sending process is described in order.
- The role of addresses and of shared rules is explained.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Send three packets through one router.
- Keep definition cards at the stations.

**For students ready for more**
- Research the difference between the two versions of IP addresses in use.
- Explain why protocols are layered, with different rules for different jobs.

## Extensions

- Find your device's IP address in settings with an adult.
- Compare the postal system with the Internet in a chart.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- The envelope model builds on Grade 4 with headers, addresses, and the idea of protocols.
- Protocols are the standard's central idea. Keep asking what would happen without the rules.
