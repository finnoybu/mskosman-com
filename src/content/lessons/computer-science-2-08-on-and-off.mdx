**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **2.CSY.1** The student will describe the characteristics of computing systems including hardware, software, input, and output.
  - d. Discuss how computers use binary code to communicate and process information.

## Prep

- Draw the dots on four cards: 8, 4, 2, and 1. Lay them in that order from left to right.
- Agree on a simple flashlight code, such as on for yes and off for no.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask yes or no questions and have the student answer only with the flashlight: on for yes, off for no. Ask, "How much could we say using only on and off?"

## Direct Instruction (6 minutes)

- Explain: "Inside a computer are huge numbers of tiny switches. Each switch can only be on or off."
- Introduce binary: "A code that uses only two values is called binary code. We write on as 1 and off as 0."
- Show the dot cards. A card face up is on (1). A card face down is off (0).
- Make the number 5: turn up the 4 card and the 1 card. Write it as 0101.
- Explain the big idea: "Letters, pictures, and sounds are all stored as long patterns of 1s and 0s. The computer processes these patterns very fast."

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. Make 3 together (2 and 1 face up). Write its pattern.
2. The student makes 6 and 9 and writes the patterns.
3. Show a pattern, such as 1010, and have the student work out the number.
4. Ask, "Why do you think computers use only two values?" Guide toward: a switch is either on or off.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

Ask the student to explain to you how a computer can store a number using only on and off. Then give one number from 1 to 15 to show with the cards and write in 1s and 0s.

Look for:
- The student says binary uses only two values.
- The student connects on and off to 1 and 0.
- The student explains that information is stored as patterns of these values.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Use only the 1, 2, and 4 cards.
- Stay with the flashlight yes and no code and build short messages.

**For students ready for more**
- Add a 16 card and make numbers up to 31.
- Invent a code where patterns of on and off stand for letters and send a short word.

## Extensions

- Send binary messages across a room with a flashlight.
- Make a bracelet with two colors of beads that spells a number in binary.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- The standard asks students to discuss the idea. Fluent conversion between number systems is not required.
- Keep returning to the main point: everything in a computer is stored with just two values.
