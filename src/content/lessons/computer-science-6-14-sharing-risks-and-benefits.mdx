**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **6.CYB.1** The student will evaluate the risks and benefits associated with sharing personal and public resources or artifacts.
  - a. Identify and explain the difference between personal and public information.
  - b. Discuss the consequences of sharing personal and confidential information online.
  - c. Evaluate risks and benefits associated with sharing information online.

## Prep

- Write twelve cards mixing personal and public information.
- Write three scenarios, such as posting a team photo with jerseys visible, sharing a project in a public gallery, and filling in a contest form with an address and birthday.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "What could someone work out about you from three photos and a username?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Distinguish personal information, which is about a specific individual, from public information, which is openly available and not private to anyone. Define confidential: meant to be seen only by specific people.
- Discuss consequences of sharing personal and confidential information: identity theft, unwanted contact, embarrassment, harm to reputation, and loss of control once it is copied.
- Introduce a simple evaluation: What is the benefit? What is the risk? How likely and how serious? Who can see it and for how long? Can it be made safer?
- Model with one scenario.

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. The student sorts the cards and explains three.
2. Evaluate the second scenario together in the table.
3. Ask, "What change would keep the benefit and lower the risk?"
4. Discuss what makes shared information hard to take back.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student evaluates the third scenario alone, reaches a decision, and justifies it with a risk and a benefit.

Look for:
- Personal, public, and confidential are distinguished.
- Consequences are realistic.
- The decision weighs likelihood and seriousness.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Sort eight cards.
- Use a two-column table with one risk and one benefit.

**For students ready for more**
- Evaluate sharing an artifact you created, such as a program, including who can copy it.
- Look at the audience settings of a platform with an adult.

## Extensions

- Review what an old post or photo reveals.
- Help a family member evaluate a sharing decision.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- The standard includes sharing artifacts and resources as well as information about yourself.
- Aim for judgment, not blanket avoidance.
