**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **2.DA.2** The student will manipulate data, create representations, and evaluate data to solve a problem.
  - a. Create charts, graphs, and models using abstraction to represent data.
  - b. Analyze data visualizations to draw conclusions.
  - c. Propose and evaluate a solution to a problem or question based on data and/or data visualization.

## Prep

- Prepare a simple bar graph that shows a problem, such as how many library books each family member returned late.
- Have grid paper ready with the bottom line drawn.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Show the recording sheet from last time. Ask, "Can you tell the answer at a glance? What would make it faster to see?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Review abstraction: a square or sticker stands for one answer. We leave out details we do not need.
- Model a bar graph: one column per category, one colored square per answer, a title, and labels.
- Mention other forms: a tally chart, a picture graph, or a model built from blocks.
- Model analysis: read the tallest and shortest bars and compare them.
- Model problem-solving with your prepared graph: "The graph shows most late books belong to one person. A solution could be a reminder note on that person's door. To evaluate it, we ask whether the data supports it and how we would know if it worked."

## Guided Practice (7 minutes)

1. The student builds a bar graph from the Lesson 13 data.
2. Ask two questions that the graph answers.
3. State a problem connected to the data, such as running out of the favorite snack.
4. Together, propose a solution and ask, "Does the data support this? What might go wrong?"

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (5 minutes)

Give the student the prepared graph and a new question about it. The student draws one conclusion, proposes a solution, and tells you one reason it is or is not a good solution.

Look for:
- The graph matches the data and has a title and labels.
- The conclusion comes from the visualization.
- The student gives a reason when judging the solution.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Build the graph with blocks before drawing it.
- Provide the graph frame and let the student fill in the bars.

**For students ready for more**
- Let one square stand for two answers.
- Propose two solutions and compare them using the data.

## Extensions

- Track a real household problem for a week, graph it, and try a solution.
- Find a graph in a newspaper or children's magazine and say what problem it could help solve.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Evaluating a solution is the new step this year. A simple "Does the data back it up?" is enough.
- Keep the student's graphs in a folder to show growth.
