**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **5.CSY.2** The student will describe an automated decision-making process employed by a computing system.
  - a. Explore decision automation and how it is used.
  - b. List outcomes of a process based on automated decisions.

## Prep

- Make the example cards.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "Who decides when the heat in a building turns on? Is a person watching the thermometer all day?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Define decision automation: "A computing system making a decision by following rules or learned patterns, without a person deciding each time."
- Describe the process: input data comes in, it is checked against rules or a model, and an action follows.
- Model with a thermostat as a table: IF temperature is below the setting THEN heat on; ELSE heat off. List the outcomes.
- Discuss why it is used: speed, consistency, and working at any hour. Discuss limits: the rules can be wrong or unfair, and unusual cases may be handled badly, so people should review important decisions.

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. The student chooses two example cards and identifies the input, the rule, and the action for each.
2. List every possible outcome for one of them.
3. Ask, "What happens if the input is wrong? Who is affected by the outcome?"
4. Decide which examples should have a person checking the result.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student describes an automated decision process of their choice, lists its outcomes in a table, and names one benefit and one risk.

Look for:
- Input, rule, and action are identified.
- All outcomes of the process are listed.
- A benefit and a risk are given.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Use the thermostat and traffic light only.
- Fill in a table with the headings provided.

**For students ready for more**
- Design the rules for an automated system at home, such as a plant waterer.
- Discuss an automated decision that might be unfair to some people and why.

## Extensions

- Spot five automated decisions during one day.
- Draw a flowchart for one.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- This connects to conditionals: an automated decision is a conditional running on real input.
- Keep examples familiar.
