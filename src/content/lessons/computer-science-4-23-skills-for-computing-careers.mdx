**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **4.IC.3** The student will examine the impact of computing technologies in the workforce.
  - a. Research and analyze the skills needed for careers in computing technology fields.

## Prep

- Bookmark child-appropriate pages describing three or four computing careers.
- Draw the chart.

## Warm-up (2 minutes)

Ask, "What are you good at that has nothing to do with computers? Could that help someone who works with computers?"

## Direct Instruction (4 minutes)

- Explain two kinds of skills. Technical skills: programming, working with data, understanding networks, design. People skills: communicating, teamwork, problem-solving, persistence, creativity.
- Model research: read a section, then note the skills in your own words.
- Explain analyzing: look across careers to see which skills appear often and why.
- Point out that skills are built over time through practice.

## Guided Practice (8 minutes)

1. The student researches two careers and fills in the chart.
2. Circle the skills that appear for both careers.
3. Ask, "Why would a programmer need to communicate well?"
4. The student marks skills they are already building.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student summarizes for you which skills are most common across computing careers and why they matter, and names one skill they would like to strengthen.

Look for:
- Skills are drawn from the sources and stated in the student's words.
- Both technical and people skills are included.
- The student identifies patterns across careers.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Research one career with the text read aloud.
- Provide a word bank of skills.

**For students ready for more**
- Research a third career and compare all three.
- Find out what classes or training build one of the skills.

## Extensions

- Interview someone in a computing career about the skills they use most.
- Set a small goal for building one skill this month.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- The standard has a single sub-item, so this lesson can go deep into research and analysis.
- Reinforce note-taking in the student's own words.
