**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **3.AP.1** The student will apply computational thinking to design algorithms to extend patterns, processes, or components of a problem.
  - a. Identify a pattern in an algorithm, process, or a problem.
  - b. Decompose a problem or task into a subset of smaller problems.

## Prep

- Choose a big task the student knows, such as planning a birthday party or cleaning a bedroom.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Say, "Clean the whole house, right now." Ask, "How does that feel? Where would you even start?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Define decompose: "To decompose a problem is to break it into smaller problems that are easier to solve one at a time."
- Model with the big task. Write the task at the top and three or four smaller problems on sticky notes underneath. Break one of those into steps.
- Explain patterns in problems: "A pattern is a part that repeats or works the same way each time." In cleaning, every room follows the same steps: pick up, wipe, sweep.
- Show why it matters: once we spot the pattern, we can solve it once and reuse the solution.

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. Give a new task, such as making lunch for four people. The student decomposes it on sticky notes.
2. Ask, "Which parts are the same for every person?" The student marks the pattern.
3. Look at the recipe or instruction sheet. Find a step or group of steps that repeats.
4. Ask, "How does knowing the pattern save work?"

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

Give the task "put on a puppet show." The student decomposes it into at least four smaller problems and points out one pattern.

Look for:
- The smaller problems together cover the whole task.
- The student identifies a part that repeats or works the same way.
- The student explains how the pattern helps.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Decompose into three parts only.
- Use a task the student did yesterday.

**For students ready for more**
- Decompose one of the smaller problems a second time.
- Find the same pattern in two different tasks.

## Extensions

- Decompose a chore and share the parts among family members.
- Find the repeating pattern in a song, a board game turn, or a knitting pattern.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Decomposition and pattern recognition are two of the main parts of computational thinking. Use both words.
- Sticky notes let the student rearrange and split problems without rewriting.
