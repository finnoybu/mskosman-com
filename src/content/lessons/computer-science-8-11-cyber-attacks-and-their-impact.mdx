**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **8.CYB.1** The student will investigate and describe ways to protect sensitive data from malware and other attacks.
  - a. Identify impacts of hacking, ransomware, scams, phishing, fake vulnerability scans and the ethical and legal concerns.
  - b. Describe how cyber-attacks can affect a computing system.

## Prep

- Make cards: hacking, ransomware, scams, phishing, fake vulnerability scans.
- Find a short, reliable news summary of one real incident.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "A pop-up says your computer has 37 viruses and to click here to fix them. What is really going on?"

## Direct Instruction (6 minutes)

- Define each. Hacking: gaining unauthorized access to a system. Ransomware: malware that locks files and demands payment. Scams: deceptions to get money or information. Phishing: messages that impersonate trusted sources. Fake vulnerability scans: false warnings that push people to install harmful software or pay for unneeded fixes.
- Describe impacts on people and organizations: lost money, stolen identity, lost data, services shut down, and damaged trust.
- Describe effects on a computing system: files encrypted or deleted, data copied out, performance slowed, settings changed, control taken by an outsider, and spread to connected systems.
- Raise legal concerns: unauthorized access and fraud are crimes with serious penalties. Raise ethical concerns: harm to people, paying ransoms, and the difference between attacking systems and testing them with permission.

## Guided Practice (7 minutes)

1. The student completes the impact chart for each attack type: who is harmed, and how the system is affected.
2. Read the incident summary and identify the attack type and its impacts.
3. Discuss: is it ever ethical to access a system without permission to show it is insecure?
4. Discuss the legal consequences for the attacker.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student describes two attack types with their impacts on people and on systems, and explains one ethical and one legal concern.

Look for:
- Attack types are defined accurately.
- Impacts on both people and systems are described.
- Ethical and legal concerns are distinguished.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Cover three attack types.
- Use the incident summary as the main example.

**For students ready for more**
- Research the role of people who test security with permission.
- Debate whether organizations should pay ransoms.

## Extensions

- Follow reporting on one incident and track its consequences.
- Explain fake vulnerability scans to a relative.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Describe what attacks do and why they matter. Do not teach how to carry them out.
- Authorized security testing is a legal career. Unauthorized access is a crime. Make the distinction clear.
