**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **8.IC.1** The student will assess the social impacts and ethical considerations of computing technologies.
  - d. Research and discuss factors that impact access and availability to computing technologies.
  - e. Discuss ethical issues around cybersecurity and networks: censorship, privacy, safety, and access.

## Prep

- Bookmark a current source on access to computing or broadband, such as national or state statistics.
- Write four dilemma cards: Should a school filter what students can see online? Should police be able to unlock a suspect's phone? Should a country be able to block a website? Should Internet access be guaranteed to everyone?

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "Two students get the same assignment. One has fast Internet and a laptop. One has a shared phone and no home connection. Is that fair? Whose problem is it?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Name factors affecting access: cost, location and infrastructure, disability and design, language, education and skills, and government policy.
- Model reading the source for evidence on one factor.
- Introduce the four ethical issues and the tension in each. Censorship: protection against freedom of information. Privacy: personal rights against security and investigation. Safety: protecting people against restricting them. Access: who should pay and who decides.
- Set discussion rules: state a position, give a reason, and state the strongest point on the other side.

## Guided Practice (8 minutes)

1. The student researches and notes evidence for two access factors.
2. Discuss one dilemma card using the discussion rules.
3. Discuss a second, with the student arguing the side they disagree with.
4. Identify who has power and who is affected in each.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student summarizes two factors that limit access, with evidence, and writes a reasoned position on one dilemma that acknowledges the opposing view.

Look for:
- Access factors are supported by evidence.
- The ethical issue is stated as a real tension.
- The position acknowledges the other side.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Research one factor.
- Discuss one dilemma.

**For students ready for more**
- Research how a specific community improved access.
- Compare how two countries handle one of the issues.

## Extensions

- Find out what access programs exist in your area.
- Hold a family discussion on one dilemma.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- These are contested questions. Present views evenhandedly and let the student reason.
- Arguing the opposing side builds understanding.
