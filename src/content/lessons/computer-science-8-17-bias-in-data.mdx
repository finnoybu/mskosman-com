**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **8.DA.2** The student will evaluate computational models to analyze patterns and make recommendations or predictions.
  - a. Define data biases within a dataset and the unintended consequences that may impact data reliability and final analysis.

## Prep

- Fill the bag with 20 tokens of one color and 20 of another, but arrange for the first color to be on top or easier to grab, such as larger pieces.
- Write three scenario cards: a survey about a new park answered only online; a health study that included only adults; a product rating based only on people who chose to write reviews.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask the student to grab ten tokens without looking and estimate what fraction of the bag is each color. Then reveal the true mix. Ask, "Why was the estimate off?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Define data bias: a systematic skew in a data set that makes it unrepresentative of what it is supposed to describe.
- Name common kinds. Sampling bias: some groups are more likely to be included. Selection or self-selection bias: only certain cases or volunteers are counted. Measurement bias: the tool or question pushes results one way. Historical bias: the data reflects past unfairness.
- Explain unintended consequences: conclusions that are wrong, decisions that are unfair to those left out, models that fail for some groups, and false confidence because the numbers look precise.
- Explain the effect on reliability: biased data gives consistently wrong answers, and collecting more of it does not fix it.

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. Identify the kind of bias in the token activity and how to remove it.
2. The student analyzes each scenario card: what is the bias, and who is missing?
3. For each, state a consequence for the final analysis.
4. Propose how the data could be collected more fairly.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student defines data bias, identifies the bias in a new scenario you describe, and explains two unintended consequences for the analysis.

Look for:
- The definition includes systematic skew.
- The kind of bias is correctly identified.
- Consequences relate to reliability and to the people affected.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Work with sampling bias only.
- Analyze one scenario in depth.

**For students ready for more**
- Examine a published survey's method and assess its possible biases.
- Explain why a very large biased sample can be worse than a small fair one.

## Extensions

- Check the next statistic you hear in an advertisement: who was asked?
- Design a fair survey for a real question.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Bias here means a skew in the data, usually unintended.
- This connects to algorithmic bias from Grade 6: biased data is a main cause.
