**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **4.IC.1** The student will identify and examine the positive and negative impacts of the prevalence of computing technologies.
  - a. Identify computing technologies that have changed Virginia’s economy.
  - b. Examine and explain how computing technologies influence and are influenced by culture.
  - c. Identify social and ethical issues related to computing devices and networks.

## Prep

- Look up two or three current examples of how computing technology is part of Virginia's economy so that what you share is accurate. Possible areas: data centers in Northern Virginia, technology used at the Port of Virginia and in shipbuilding, and technology on farms.
- Note one example close to where you live.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "What jobs do people have in our part of Virginia? Which of them depend on computers?"

## Direct Instruction (6 minutes)

- Define economy: how people in a place make, sell, and buy things and earn a living.
- Share the examples you looked up and place a sticky note on the map for each. Explain how each changed work or created jobs.
- Review culture and influence in both directions, using a Virginia example such as historic sites offering virtual tours, or local festivals and teams shared online.
- Identify issues tied to devices and networks. Social: not every community has fast Internet. Ethical: how companies use the information they collect, and the land, power, and water that large computing facilities use.

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. The student explains how one technology changed work in Virginia.
2. Together, find one example of technology and culture influencing each other in your community.
3. Sort three issues as social or ethical and say who is affected.
4. Ask, "What is a positive impact and a negative impact of that same technology?"

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student names two technologies that have changed Virginia's economy, gives one culture example, and identifies one social or ethical issue with their own view on it.

Look for:
- Examples are real and connected to Virginia.
- The culture example shows influence.
- The issue is explained in terms of the people affected.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Focus on one technology near your home.
- Use pictures of local workplaces.

**For students ready for more**
- Research how many people in your region work in technology jobs.
- Interview a local worker about how technology changed their industry.

## Extensions

- Visit or virtually tour a Virginia workplace that relies on technology.
- Add the examples to a Virginia Studies notebook.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Facts about the state's economy change. Check them before the lesson.
- This lesson pairs well with Virginia Studies in history.
