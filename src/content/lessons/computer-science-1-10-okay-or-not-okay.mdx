**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **1.CYB.1** The student will demonstrate safe and responsible use of computing technologies.
  - a. Describe safe and responsible uses of computing technologies based on the school rules and acceptable use policy (AUP).
  - d. Classify appropriate and inappropriate uses of technology at school or at home.
  - e. Explain the consequences of inappropriate uses of computing technologies.

## Prep

- Write or update your technology rules in short sentences.
- Write 10 situation cards, about half appropriate and half not. Examples: asking before downloading a game; using a device at the table during dinner; sending a kind message to a cousin; clicking on a pop-up; using someone else's device without asking; stopping when the timer rings.
- Decide what the real consequences are in your home when a rule is broken.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "What is one of our technology rules? Why do we have it?" Then ask, "What might happen if nobody followed it?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Review the rules. Remind the student that a written set of technology rules is called an acceptable use policy.
- Define the two words: "Appropriate means it follows the rules and is safe and kind. Inappropriate means it breaks a rule, or it could hurt someone or cause a problem."
- Model with two cards. Read each aloud, place it under a header, and say which rule it connects to.
- Introduce consequences: "A consequence is what happens because of a choice."
- Give examples of consequences of inappropriate use: someone's feelings are hurt; the device gets damaged; private information gets out; you see something that upsets you; you lose device time.
- Also name a positive consequence: "When we use technology responsibly, people trust us to use it more."

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. The student draws a card, reads or listens to it, and classifies it.
2. For each inappropriate card, ask, "What could happen because of this?"
3. For each appropriate card, ask, "What makes this a safe or responsible choice?"
4. Continue through at least six cards.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student classifies the remaining cards alone. Then ask the student to describe two responsible ways they use technology and to explain one consequence of an inappropriate use.

Look for:
- Cards are classified correctly with a reason.
- The student describes responsible use in their own words.
- The student connects an inappropriate use to a specific consequence.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Use six cards with clear-cut situations.
- Add a simple drawing to each card.
- Talk through the consequence together before the student answers.

**For students ready for more**
- Include "it depends" cards, such as watching a video (which video, when, and who said yes?).
- The student rewrites an inappropriate situation so that it becomes appropriate.

## Extensions

- Add a rule the student suggests to the family rules.
- Act out a situation with toys and show a better choice.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- The standard refers to school rules and an acceptable use policy (AUP). At home, your written rules serve that purpose. If the student is in a program with its own AUP, use it.
- Consequences should be real and calm, not threats. Include natural consequences as well as household ones.
- Lesson 11 covers demonstrating safe behavior and reporting.
