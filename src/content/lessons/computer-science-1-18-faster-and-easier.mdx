**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **1.IC.3** The student will compare and contrast ways people complete tasks with and without computing technologies.
  - c. Describe how the appropriate use of computing technologies can improve efficiency.
  - d. List computing technologies used in various careers.

## Prep

- Write ten addition facts in a list.
- Make six career cards with drawings or pictures.
- For each career, note one or two computing technologies that worker uses so you can prompt the student.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "What does it mean to do something fast? What does it mean to do it well?" Then ask, "Is fast always better?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Define: "Efficient means getting a job done well without wasting time or effort."
- Run a quick experiment. You add the ten facts by counting on your fingers while the student times you. Then use the calculator while the student times you again. Compare the times.
- Draw out the idea: "The calculator made the task more efficient."
- Add the key word from the standard: "This only works with appropriate use. If I press the wrong keys or use the calculator to play, it does not help. And I still need to know what the numbers mean."
- Turn to careers: "Workers use computing technologies to be efficient."
- Show two career cards and name a technology for each: a nurse uses a computer to keep patient records and a digital thermometer. A farmer uses a weather app and a tractor that steers with a map.

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. For each remaining career card, ask, "What computing technology might this worker use?" Prompt as needed.
2. Write a list together: career on the left, technologies on the right.
3. For three careers, ask, "How does that technology make the work more efficient?"
4. Ask, "What would be an inappropriate way to use that tool at work?"

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student chooses two careers that were not on the cards, lists one computing technology for each, and explains how one of those technologies improves efficiency when used appropriately.

Look for:
- The student explains efficiency in their own words.
- The student links appropriate use to getting the benefit.
- The student lists technologies for at least four careers in total.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Say "faster and easier" alongside "efficient."
- Use careers of people the student knows.
- Act out the job with and without the technology.

**For students ready for more**
- Find a career where the technology changed the job a great deal.
- Ask what could go wrong at work if a technology were used carelessly.

## Extensions

- Ask two grown-ups what technology they use at work and add them to the list.
- Time a home task done two ways, such as finding a word in a paper dictionary and on a device.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- The calculator comparison is a demonstration by you. First graders should still learn their facts.
- "Appropriate use" is a required part of the standard, so name it directly.
- Kindergarten described how careers use technology. Grade 1 asks for a list across various careers, so aim for breadth.
