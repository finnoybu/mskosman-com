**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **K.AP.1** The student will apply computational thinking to identify patterns and sort items into categories based on an attribute.
  - b. Compare two objects and list attributes they have in common.
  - c. Sort and classify concrete objects based on one attribute.

## Prep

- Choose 6 to 8 pairs of objects so that each pair shares at least two attributes and differs in at least one.
- Set aside 10 to 12 blocks or counters in exactly two colors for the pattern work.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Review: "What is an attribute?" Hold up one object and have the student name two of its attributes.

Then hold up two objects that share a color. Ask, "What is the same about these two?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Explain: "When we compare two objects, we look for what is the same and what is different."
- Model with one pair. Say what they have in common first: "Both are red. Both are small." Then what is different: "One is round and one is square."
- Draw two simple columns on paper labeled with a matching sign and a not-matching sign of your choice. Record one shared attribute as a quick drawing or color mark.
- Introduce patterns: lay out blocks red, blue, red, blue, red, blue. Say, "A pattern is something that repeats. I can see the color attribute repeating: red, blue, red, blue."

## Guided Practice (5 minutes)

1. Give the student a new pair of objects. Together, list at least two attributes they have in common.
2. Repeat with a second pair. Let the student lead.
3. Build a new two-color pattern together. Read it aloud while touching each block.
4. Ask, "What comes next? How do you know?"

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (5 minutes)

- Hand the student one more pair. Ask them to tell you everything the two objects have in common.
- Lay out a short repeating pattern with one block missing at the end. The student adds the next block and says the pattern aloud.
- Finally, the student sorts the blocks into two groups by color.

Look for:
- The student lists at least two shared attributes for a pair.
- The student identifies what repeats in the pattern.
- The sort uses one attribute only.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Use pairs that share one obvious attribute, such as color.
- Offer a choice: "Are they the same color or the same shape?"
- Keep patterns to two colors and read them together in rhythm.

**For students ready for more**
- Compare objects that share three or more attributes.
- Use a pattern with three parts, or a pattern based on size or shape, not color.

## Extensions

- Compare two family members' shoes, two fruits, or two toys and list what they have in common.
- Find patterns on clothing, floor tiles, or fences during a walk.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Kindergartners usually find differences faster than similarities. Keep steering back to "What is the same?" because that is what the standard asks for.
- Recording can be drawings or color marks. Writing words is not required.
- The standard asks students to identify patterns, not create complex ones. Keep patterns short and clear.
