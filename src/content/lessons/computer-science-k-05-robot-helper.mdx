**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **K.AP.2** The student will plan and implement algorithms that include sequential order.
  - d. Create and test a sequential algorithm emphasizing beginning, middle, and end.
  - e. Create and test a sequential algorithm to accomplish a predetermined task.

## Prep

- Draw arrows on 4 to 6 cards or sticky notes: at least three forward, one turn left, one turn right.
- Clear a floor space, mark a start spot with tape, and pick a target.
- Walk the path once yourself so you know the target can be reached in four or five cards.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Tell the student, "Today I am a robot. Robots only do exactly what they are told, one step at a time."

Stand still and ask the student to get you to clap your hands. Follow the words literally. If the student says "Clap," clap once and stop.

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Set the task: "The robot must get from the start spot to the target." Point out both.
- Show the arrow cards and act out each one: one step forward, turn left, turn right.
- Model building an algorithm: lay three or four cards in a row on the floor or table, reading them left to right.
- Name the parts: "My first card is the beginning. My last card is the end. The cards between are the middle."
- Test it. Walk the cards exactly as laid out. Stop short of the target on purpose.
- Say, "My algorithm did not work yet. That is okay. I can change a step and test again." Add or swap a card and retest.

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. Move the target to a new place.
2. Together, lay out cards to reach it. The student chooses each card; you ask, "What will the robot do on this step?"
3. The student reads the cards aloud while you walk them as the robot.
4. If the robot misses the target, find the step to change together and test again.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (6 minutes)

Move the target once more. The student builds an algorithm alone, then tests it with you as the robot. Allow one round of fixing.

Look for:
- The cards are placed in a clear order from beginning to end.
- The student tests the algorithm instead of assuming it works.
- When the test fails, the student changes a step and tries again.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Use only forward cards and a target in a straight line.
- Let the student walk the path first, then build the cards to match what they did.
- Limit the algorithm to three cards.

**For students ready for more**
- Add an obstacle the robot must go around.
- Switch roles: you write the algorithm with one mistake in it, and the student finds and fixes it.

## Extensions

- Write an algorithm for a toy car or stuffed animal to travel across a tabletop grid made with tape.
- Give "robot" directions for a real chore, such as setting one place at the table.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Be a strict robot. Doing only what the cards say is what makes the test meaningful.
- Left and right are hard at this age. A sticker on one hand, or pointing arrows, removes that barrier so the focus stays on ordering steps.
- Praise the fixing as much as the success. Creating and testing are both named in the standard.
- No device is needed. The same thinking applies later when the student programs on a screen.
