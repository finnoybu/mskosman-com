**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **3.IC.1** The student will identify and examine the positive and negative impacts of the prevalence of computing technologies.
  - a. Identify computing technologies that have changed the world.
  - b. Examine and explain how computing technologies influence and are influenced by culture.
  - c. Identify social and ethical issues related to the use of computing technologies.

## Prep

- Make the five cards.
- Think of one way your own family's traditions or habits have changed because of technology.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "Imagine waking up tomorrow with no Internet anywhere. What would be different about your day?"

## Direct Instruction (6 minutes)

- Introduce the five technologies. For each, say one way it changed how people live.
- Define culture: the ways a group of people live, including language, food, music, celebrations, and how they communicate.
- Explain influence in both directions. Technology influences culture: families celebrate birthdays over video calls. Culture influences technology: keyboards and apps are made in many languages, and emoji include foods and holidays from many cultures.
- Define the two issue types. Social issues affect how people live together, such as not everyone having access to devices or the Internet. Ethical issues are questions of right and wrong, such as whether it is fair to share a photo of someone without asking.

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. The student chooses one card and describes the world before and after.
2. Together, find one example of technology shaping your family's culture and one of culture shaping technology.
3. Sort four situations as social or ethical: a friend has no Internet at home; copying someone's work; posting an embarrassing video; an app only in one language.
4. Ask for each, "Who is affected? What would be fair?"

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student names two technologies that changed the world and how, gives one example of technology and culture influencing each other, and identifies one social or ethical issue with a reason it matters.

Look for:
- Changes described are real and specific.
- The culture example shows influence in at least one direction.
- The student explains why the issue matters to people.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Use one technology and tell its before-and-after story.
- Say "fair or not fair" and "affects everyone" alongside the new terms.

**For students ready for more**
- Research the inventor or history of one technology.
- Debate both sides of an ethical question, such as whether children should have phones.

## Extensions

- Ask an older relative how one of the five technologies changed their life.
- Make a timeline of the five technologies.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Positive and negative impacts should both come up. Ask for one of each.
- Keep ethical discussions open. The goal is recognizing the issue, not settling it.
