**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **K.AP.2** The student will plan and implement algorithms that include sequential order.
  - c. Create a design document to illustrate thoughts, ideas, and stories in a sequential manner.
  - d. Create and test a sequential algorithm emphasizing beginning, middle, and end.

## Prep

- Fold or rule two sheets of paper into three boxes each.
- Choose a story or rhyme you can sketch in three quick drawings.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Review: "What do we call steps done in order?" (An algorithm.) Ask the student to tell the steps for washing hands using first, next, last.

Then ask, "How could we remember our steps if we could not say them out loud?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Show the paper with three boxes. Say, "Before people build something, they often draw a plan. A plan on paper is called a design document."
- Point to each box and name it: beginning, middle, end.
- Model with a familiar story or rhyme. Draw one quick sketch in each box while saying what happens at the beginning, in the middle, and at the end.
- Touch each box in order and retell the story from the drawings.
- Explain testing: "To test a plan, we follow it exactly and see if it works."

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. Choose a short task together, such as putting the toy to bed (pick up the toy, lay it down, cover it).
2. On a fresh three-box sheet, decide together what goes in the beginning, middle, and end. The student draws; you may label.
3. Test it: you follow the drawings exactly with the toy while the student watches.
4. Ask, "Did it work? Is anything missing or out of order?" Fix the drawing if needed and test again.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (5 minutes)

Give the student a new three-box sheet. Ask them to draw their own idea or a short story with a beginning, a middle, and an end. When finished, the student "reads" the plan to you in order, and you act it out or retell it to test it.

Look for:
- Three drawings that show three different moments.
- The drawings are in an order that makes sense.
- After the test, the student can say whether the plan worked.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Provide three pre-drawn pictures to cut out and glue in order as the plan.
- Do the drawing yourself while the student tells you what belongs in each box.
- Use a routine the student does every day.

**For students ready for more**
- Add a fourth box and a fourth step.
- Trade plans: the student follows a plan you drew without any spoken help.

## Extensions

- Keep a stack of three-box sheets handy for retelling a book, a field trip, or a recipe.
- Draw a plan for a block tower, build it, and compare the tower to the plan.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Drawing skill does not matter. Stick figures and scribbles are fine as long as the student can explain each box.
- "Design document" is the standard's term for the drawn plan. Use the phrase, but do not expect the student to memorize it.
- Testing is the important new idea. Follow the plan literally so that mistakes show up, and treat fixing them as a normal part of the work.
