**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **1.IC.2** The student will describe tasks and activities that use screens and categorize them based on their use.
  - a. Identify daily routines and activities that can be completed with or without screens.
  - b. Classify the different uses of screen time as learning, entertainment, or communication.
- **1.IC.1** The student will describe how computing technologies impact daily tasks and communication.
  - c. Describe healthy habits for using computing technologies.

## Prep

- Write or draw one activity per card: 6 screen activities your family really does (a reading app, a cartoon, a video call, a math game, a message to a relative, a movie) and 6 daily routines (brushing teeth, eating breakfast, reading a book, playing outside, getting dressed, bedtime).

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask the student to tell you about their day so far, from waking up until now. As they talk, hold up a finger each time a screen was involved.

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Sort the routine cards first: "Some daily routines never need a screen, like brushing teeth. Some can be done either way, like reading a story."
- Introduce the three uses of screen time with the header cards:
  - **Learning**: using a screen to find out or practice something.
  - **Entertainment**: using a screen for fun.
  - **Communication**: using a screen to talk with or send something to another person.
- Place one screen card under each header and explain why.
- Teach healthy habits:
  - Balance screen time with moving, playing, reading, and being with people.
  - Take breaks and rest your eyes.
  - Sit up and hold the screen at a comfortable distance.
  - Turn screens off at meals and before bed.
- Connect the ideas: "Knowing what we are using a screen for helps us decide how much is right."

## Guided Practice (5 minutes)

1. The student sorts the routine cards into "no screen needed" and "could be either way."
2. The student classifies the remaining screen cards as learning, entertainment, or communication.
3. Talk about any card that could fit two groups.
4. Ask, "Which healthy habit goes with watching a movie? With a long video call?"

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

Name three new screen activities. The student classifies each. Then the student names two routines that do not need a screen and describes three healthy habits.

Look for:
- Screen uses are classified with a sensible reason.
- The student identifies routines done with and without screens.
- The student describes healthy habits in their own words.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Use two categories first: fun and not just for fun.
- Use photos of the family's real apps and activities.
- Choose one healthy habit to practice all week.

**For students ready for more**
- Tally the family's screen activities for one day by category and graph them, using Lesson 14.
- Discuss an activity that is both learning and entertainment.

## Extensions

- Make a balanced day picture with screen and non-screen activities.
- Choose a "screens off" signal for the family, such as a timer or a song.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Some activities fit more than one category. Accept any classification the student can explain.
- Avoid labeling entertainment as bad. The point is noticing the purpose and keeping a balance.
- Your family decides its own limits. This lesson supplies the vocabulary for talking about them.
