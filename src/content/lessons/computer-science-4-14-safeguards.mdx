**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **4.CYB.2** The student will identify and investigate best practices to safeguard information shared online and through online platforms.
  - c. Investigate and communicate best practices to limit unauthorized access to information on a computing device.
  - d. Demonstrate proper use and protection of personal passwords.
  - e. List methods used to safeguard online information.

## Prep

- Find where your device shows its screen lock and update settings so you can show them without changing anything.
- Have a practice account or a pretend sign-in sheet for the password demonstration.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "If you left a tablet on a park bench, what could a stranger do with it? What would stop them?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Define unauthorized access: someone getting into a device or account without permission.
- Present best practices for a device: use a screen lock, lock it when you step away, keep software updated, install only approved apps, and do not leave it unattended.
- Present proper password use: make it strong, keep it private, never reuse it across accounts, type it where others cannot watch, and sign out on shared devices.
- List other safeguards: two-step sign-in, privacy settings, checking that a website is the real one, and backing up important files.

## Guided Practice (7 minutes)

1. Look at the device's lock and update settings together. The student explains what each one protects.
2. The student demonstrates entering a practice password properly: screen shielded, not said aloud, then signing out.
3. Together, list methods that safeguard online information. Aim for six.
4. Plan a one-page guide for the family.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student writes and illustrates the guide, "Five Ways We Keep Our Devices and Accounts Safe," and presents it to you.

Look for:
- The guide communicates accurate practices.
- The student demonstrates password habits correctly.
- Several safeguards beyond passwords are listed.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Make a three-item guide.
- Practice locking and unlocking the device.

**For students ready for more**
- Research what two-step sign-in is and explain it in the guide.
- Check with a parent which family accounts could use a stronger safeguard.

## Extensions

- Post the guide near the family computer.
- Present it to a grandparent or younger sibling.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- "Communicate" is part of the standard, so the guide and presentation matter.
- You change the settings. The student observes and explains.
