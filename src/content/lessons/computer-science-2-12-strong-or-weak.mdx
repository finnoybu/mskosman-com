**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **2.CYB.2** The student will explain the importance of using passwords to protect private information.
  - a. Identify and classify passwords as strong or weak.
  - b. Explain how a strong password helps protect the privacy of information.
  - c. Explain the risk of sharing passwords.

## Prep

- Write ten pretend passwords on cards. Weak examples: 1234, password, a pet's name, abc. Strong examples: longer mixes of unrelated words, numbers, and symbols, such as PurpleTaco7!Kite.
- Use only made-up examples. Never use a real password.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Play a guessing game. Think of a pretend password that is your pet's name or 1234 and let the student guess. Then say, "That was easy. Why?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Review: a password is the key that protects an account and the private information inside it.
- Define weak: short, common, or easy to guess from knowing you, such as a name or birthday.
- Define strong: long, hard to guess, and a mix of letters, numbers, and symbols. Several unrelated words together make a long password that is easier to remember.
- Explain protection: "A strong password takes far too long to guess, so other people cannot get into your information."
- Explain the risk of sharing: "If someone else knows your password, they can see your private information, pretend to be you, or change your things. Even a friend might pass it on by mistake."

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. The student sorts the ten cards and gives a reason for each.
2. Take one weak password and improve it together.
3. Ask, "How does this stronger one protect the information better?"
4. Discuss two situations: a friend asks for your password to help you in a game, and a pop-up asks you to type your password.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

Show three new made-up passwords. The student classifies each. Then the student explains what could happen if a password were shared and who is allowed to know theirs.

Look for:
- Passwords are classified correctly with reasons about length and guessability.
- The student links a strong password to keeping information private.
- The student names a real risk of sharing.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Sort by one rule only: long or short.
- Use picture clues on the cards for what makes a password guessable.

**For students ready for more**
- Create a practice passphrase from four unrelated words and test whether you can guess it.
- Explain why using the same password everywhere is risky.

## Extensions

- Make a "strong password recipe" poster.
- Ask a grown-up, without seeing any password, whether they use long ones.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Grade 1 covered what passwords are for. Grade 2 adds judging their strength and the risk of sharing.
- Trusted grown-ups such as parents are the exception to the no-sharing rule at this age.
