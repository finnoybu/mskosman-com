**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **6.CSY.3** The student will identify and describe Artificial Intelligence (AI).
  - a. Define artificial intelligence and identify the characteristics of artificial intelligence.
  - b. Describe how AI technologies generate information or automate decision and how people interact with AI technologies.

## Prep

- Write ten example cards, some AI and some not: a voice assistant, a calculator, a spam filter, a light switch, a movie recommendation list, a chatbot, a microwave timer, a map app predicting traffic, a photo app that groups faces, a digital clock.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "What makes something artificial intelligence? Is a calculator intelligent?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Define artificial intelligence: computing systems that perform tasks that normally need human intelligence, such as recognizing images or speech, understanding language, making predictions, and making decisions.
- Give characteristics: it learns from data, finds patterns, makes predictions or decisions, and can improve with more data. It does not understand or feel as people do.
- Explain the two things it does. Generating information: producing text, images, or recommendations based on patterns in training data. Automating decisions: choosing an action from input, such as filtering a message.
- Describe how people interact with AI: speaking, typing prompts, clicking recommendations, and through data collected as they use services, often without noticing.

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. The student sorts the example cards into AI and not AI, with reasons.
2. For three AI cards, state the input, what the AI does, and the output.
3. Classify each as generating information or automating a decision.
4. Ask, "When have you interacted with AI without realizing it?"

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student defines AI, lists three characteristics, and describes one AI technology: what it generates or decides and how a person interacts with it.

Look for:
- The definition and characteristics are accurate.
- Generation and decision automation are distinguished.
- The interaction is described from the user's side.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Sort six cards.
- Focus on one example in depth.

**For students ready for more**
- Explain why AI output can sound confident and still be wrong.
- Compare a rule-based program with one that learns from data.

## Extensions

- Keep a one-day list of interactions with AI.
- Ask a family member how they use AI tools.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Use precise language: AI finds patterns and predicts. It does not know things.
- An adult should operate any AI tool used for demonstration and check the terms for age requirements.
