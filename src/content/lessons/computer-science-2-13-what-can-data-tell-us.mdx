**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **2.DA.1** The student will analyze data to make decisions with or without a computing device.
  - a. Collect and record numeric and non-numeric data and describe possible patterns.
  - b. Create questions that can and cannot be answered by the data.
  - c. Analyze data to draw conclusions and make decisions.

## Prep

- Decide on a small survey with a real decision attached, such as which snack to buy for a family game night.
- If few people are available, prepare answer slips from relatives ahead of time.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "If we wanted to choose a snack everyone would like, how could we decide fairly?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Review: numeric data is numbers, and non-numeric data is words or categories. Point to one column of each kind on the recording sheet.
- Model recording one person's answers in a row.
- Explain patterns: something that shows up again and again, or the most and least common answers.
- Teach two kinds of questions. Questions the data can answer: "Which snack was chosen most?" Questions the data cannot answer: "Why do they like it?" or "What will they want next year?"
- Explain: "We can only answer a question if the data we collected has that information."

## Guided Practice (7 minutes)

1. Collect and record the data together.
2. The student describes two patterns.
3. Together, write two questions the data can answer and two it cannot. For each one it cannot answer, say what data would be needed.
4. Answer the questions that can be answered.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (5 minutes)

The student writes one more question of each kind, draws a conclusion from the data, and makes the decision: which snack should we buy, and why?

Look for:
- Both kinds of data are recorded accurately.
- The student tells apart questions the data can and cannot answer.
- The decision is supported by the data.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Use one non-numeric column only.
- Offer question starters: "How many..." and "Which..."

**For students ready for more**
- Collect two numeric columns and look for a pattern between them.
- Ask whether the decision would change if more people were surveyed.

## Extensions

- Use data to make another real decision, such as which game to play or which book to read aloud.
- Record the same question again next month and compare.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- A real decision makes the data matter. Follow through on what the data says.
- The idea that some questions cannot be answered by a data set is new this year and worth extra time.
