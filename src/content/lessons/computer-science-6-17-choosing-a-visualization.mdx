**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **6.DA.2** The student will utilize computational tools to visualize and evaluate data.
  - a. Identify different types of visual representations of data.
  - b. Compare various visual representations and identify when each should be used.

## Prep

- Collect or sketch one example of each type.
- Write six questions, each best answered by a different type, such as how a value changed over a year, or what share of a budget goes to each category.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Show a pie chart with fifteen thin slices. Ask, "What is this trying to tell you? Is it working?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Introduce the types and their purposes. Bar graph: compare amounts across categories. Line graph: show change over time. Pie chart: show parts of a whole, with few categories. Scatter plot: show the relationship between two numeric variables. Histogram: show how values are distributed across ranges. Map: show data by location.
- Give the deciding questions: What kind of data is it, categories or numbers? Is time involved? Am I comparing, showing change, showing parts, or showing a relationship?
- Show a poor match and explain why it misleads or confuses.
- Start the reference chart: type, best for, avoid when.

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. The student matches each question card to a visualization and justifies it.
2. Compare a bar graph and a pie chart of the same data.
3. Compare a line graph and a bar graph of data over time.
4. The student completes the reference chart.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

Give three new data situations. The student names the best visualization for each and explains why another type would be worse.

Look for:
- Types are identified by name.
- Choices are justified by the kind of data and the purpose.
- The student recognizes a poor match.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Work with three types: bar, line, and pie.
- Use the reference chart while matching.

**For students ready for more**
- Find a misleading graph, such as one with a cut-off axis, and explain the problem.
- Suggest a better visualization for the fifteen-slice pie chart.

## Extensions

- Collect visualizations from news sources and label each type.
- Critique a chart from a textbook or advertisement.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- The choice of chart is an argument about what matters in the data.
- Keep the reference chart for the next two lessons.
