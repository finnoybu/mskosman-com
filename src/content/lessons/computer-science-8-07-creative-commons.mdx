**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **8.AP.4** The student will incorporate work from others into programs and projects.
  - a. Explain the role of Creative Commons licensing for the use and modification or “remixing” of information.
  - b. Utilize Creative Commons assets in a programming project.

## Prep

- Find a source of Creative Commons licensed media and check how it shows each item's license.
- Make a reference card of the four elements: BY (give credit), SA (share adaptations under the same license), NC (non-commercial use only), ND (no derivatives, meaning no changes).

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "You want others to use your photo, but you want credit and you do not want it sold. How could you tell the whole world that, without answering every request?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Explain the role: under ordinary copyright, all rights are reserved and each use needs permission. Creative Commons licenses let creators grant some permissions in advance, in a standard form anyone can read.
- Teach the four elements and how they combine, such as BY, BY-SA, BY-NC, and BY-ND.
- Define remixing: changing or combining existing work to make something new. ND licenses do not allow it. SA licenses require the remix to carry the same license.
- Teach attribution for these works: title, author, source, and license.

## Guided Practice (8 minutes)

1. The student finds two assets and reads each license.
2. Decide for each: may it be changed? used commercially? what must be done in return?
3. Add one asset to the programming project.
4. Write the attribution in the project's credits.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student adds a second Creative Commons asset, attributes it, and explains what its license allows and what it would forbid.

Look for:
- The role of Creative Commons is explained.
- License elements are interpreted correctly.
- Assets are attributed with title, author, source, and license.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Use assets with the simplest license, BY.
- Interpret one license together.

**For students ready for more**
- Remix an asset permitted by its license and license the result correctly.
- Choose a Creative Commons license for your own project and explain why.

## Extensions

- Find the license on a website, a photo, and a piece of music.
- License one of your own creations.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Creative Commons builds on copyright. It does not replace it.
- Checking for ND before changing an asset is the step most often missed.
