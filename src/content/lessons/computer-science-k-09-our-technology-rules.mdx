**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **K.CYB.1** The student will demonstrate safe and responsible use of computing technologies.
  - a. Identify safe and responsible uses of computing technologies based on the school rules and acceptable use policy (AUP).
  - b. Demonstrate safe and responsible uses of computing technologies.
  - c. Communicate the process(es) for reporting behaviors that do not comply with school rules or acceptable use policy.

## Prep

- Write 3 to 5 technology rules for your home or program in short sentences the student can understand.
- Decide who the student's trusted grown-ups are.
- Choose the one app or activity the student will open during the demonstration.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "What is one rule we have at the playground or in the car? Why do we have it?" Guide the student to the idea that rules keep people safe.

Say, "We have rules for technology too."

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Explain: "Schools and learning programs write down their technology rules. That written set of rules is called an acceptable use policy. We can call ours our technology rules."
- Read your rules aloud one at a time, pointing to each. Typical examples: ask a grown-up before using a device; use only the apps and sites a grown-up has chosen; be kind in anything you say or send; stop when time is up.
- For each rule, say in one sentence why it keeps people safe.
- Explain "responsible": doing the right thing even when no one is watching.
- Teach reporting: "If you see something that breaks a rule, or something on a screen that feels wrong, you stop and tell a trusted grown-up right away. Telling is not tattling. It keeps everyone safe."
- Name who the trusted grown-ups are for this student.

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. Play "Thumbs up, thumbs down." Describe a short situation, and the student shows whether it follows the rules. Example: "Maya asked her dad before opening the tablet." "Sam kept playing after the timer rang."
2. For each thumbs down, ask, "Which rule was broken? What should happen next?"
3. With the device, the student demonstrates one rule, such as asking first and then opening only the chosen app.
4. Practice the reporting words together: "I saw something that is not okay. Can you help?"

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

Give two new situations, one safe and one not. The student tells you which is which and why. Then ask, "What do you do if someone is not following the technology rules?" Finally, the student shows one rule in action on the device.

Look for:
- The student identifies safe and unsafe uses based on your stated rules.
- The student follows a rule while actually using the device.
- The student names a trusted grown-up and says they would tell that person.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Limit the list to three rules, each with a picture.
- Act out situations with toys so the student can watch before judging.
- Rehearse the reporting sentence several times.

**For students ready for more**
- The student invents situations for you to judge.
- Ask why a rule exists and what could happen without it.

## Extensions

- Make a technology rules poster together and hang it near the device.
- Review one rule at the start of each screen session for a week.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- The standard refers to school rules and an acceptable use policy (AUP). In a homeschool or tutoring setting, your own written rules serve that role. If the student is enrolled in a program or co-op with its own AUP, use that document.
- Write the rules before the lesson. The student needs something specific to measure behavior against.
- Respond calmly whenever the student reports something. A harsh reaction teaches them to stop telling.
- Demonstrating is part of the standard, so include the hands-on step.
