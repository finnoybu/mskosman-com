**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **K.CSY.1** The student will identify the purpose and components of a computing device.
  - e. Demonstrate proper use of computing devices.
- **K.CSY.2** The student will identify when a computing device might not work as expected.
  - a. Identify a problem with a computing device when it is not working as expected.
  - b. Explain what to do when a computing device is not working as expected.

## Prep

- Charge the device.
- Turn its volume all the way down and have a sound or short video ready to play.
- Fill the cup and decide where it will sit, well away from the device.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "How do we take care of a book?" Then, "How do you think we should take care of a computing device?" Listen to the student's ideas.

## Direct Instruction (6 minutes)

Model proper use, naming each habit as you do it:

- **Clean, dry hands.** Food and drinks stay away. Show the cup of water placed far from the device.
- **Carry with two hands** and walk.
- **Set it on a flat table**, not the edge.
- **Gentle touches.** Tap and press softly.
- **Ask a grown-up** before using a device.

Then introduce problems: "Sometimes a device does not do what we expect. That is an uh-oh moment."

Show or describe a few: the screen is black, there is no sound, the screen is frozen and will not respond, the battery picture is empty.

Teach what to do, in order:

1. Stop. Hands off.
2. Look. What is happening?
3. Tell a grown-up what you see.

Add: "We do not bang, shake, or keep pressing buttons."

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. The student practices carrying the device to the table with two hands and setting it down.
2. The student shows a gentle tap and a gentle press.
3. Role-play: turn the volume all the way down before the lesson, then try to play a sound. Ask, "Is it working the way we expect? What is the problem?"
4. The student practices the three steps: stop, look, tell. Then fix the volume together.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

Ask the student to show you, from start to finish, how to get ready and use the device properly. Then describe a new uh-oh moment ("The screen went black") and ask, "What is the problem? What do you do?"

Look for:
- The student carries, places, and touches the device properly without reminders.
- The student states what is wrong in their own words.
- The student explains the stop, look, tell steps.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Practice one care habit at a time over several days.
- Use a picture card for each of the three uh-oh steps.
- Practice carrying with a book before using the real device.

**For students ready for more**
- With your permission, the student tries one simple check before telling you, such as looking to see whether the device is plugged in or the volume is turned down.
- The student teaches the care habits to a sibling or a stuffed animal.

## Extensions

- Make a small care poster with drawings and keep it where the device is stored.
- Use "uh-oh moment" language for other things that do not work as expected, like a flashlight with no batteries.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- The standard asks the student to demonstrate proper use, so watch them do it. Telling you the rules is not enough.
- Stage only safe problems (volume down, device asleep). Never create a real fault.
- At this grade the expected response to a problem is to recognize it and get help. Fixing it is not required.
- Your own household rules about devices can be added here. They are the focus of Lesson 9.
