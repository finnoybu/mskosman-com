**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **K.NI.1** The student will describe how people can communicate with others by using connected computing devices and the Internet.
  - a. Identify ways to communicate with others using connected computing devices and the Internet.
  - b. Describe the benefits of communicating with others using connected computing devices and the Internet.

## Prep

- Make the string phone: poke a small hole in the bottom of each cup, thread a string 6 to 10 feet long through both, and knot each end inside its cup.
- Optional: arrange for a family member to receive a short message or video call during the lesson.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "How do you talk to someone in the same room? How could you talk to someone who lives far away?"

Hand the student one cup of the string phone and hold the other. Pull the string tight and say hello. Ask, "What is connecting us?"

## Direct Instruction (4 minutes)

- Explain: "Computing devices can be connected to each other, a little like our cups and string. When devices are connected, they can send messages, pictures, and voices back and forth."
- Explain the Internet simply: "The Internet connects devices all around the world."
- Name ways people communicate using connected devices:
  - Video calls, where you see and hear the person.
  - Text messages.
  - Email, which is a letter sent from one device to another.
  - Sharing photos.
- Describe benefits: "It is fast. We can talk to people who are far away. We can see faces and share pictures. We can get help or learn something from someone who is not nearby."
- Connect to safety: "We only communicate online with people our trusted grown-ups have said are okay."

## Guided Practice (5 minutes)

1. Ask, "Who in our family lives far away? How do we talk to them?"
2. Together, list three ways your family uses connected devices to communicate. Draw a quick picture for each.
3. For each one, ask, "Why is that helpful?"
4. Optional: with you operating the device, send a short message or picture to a family member, or place a brief video call, so the student sees it work.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

Ask the student to draw themselves communicating with someone using a connected device. Then ask:

1. "What way of communicating did you draw?"
2. "Tell me one other way people can communicate using connected devices and the Internet."
3. "Why is it good to be able to communicate this way?"

Look for:
- The student names at least two ways to communicate with connected devices.
- The student gives at least one benefit.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Focus on one way the student has experienced, such as a video call with a relative.
- Use the string phone again to act out sending and receiving.
- Offer benefit choices: "Is it fast or slow? Can we see Grandma or not?"

**For students ready for more**
- Compare a paper letter with an email: what is the same and what is different?
- Ask what happens when a device is not connected.

## Extensions

- With a trusted grown-up, record a short video or voice message for a relative.
- Draw a map with your home and a faraway relative's home, and draw a line showing the connection.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Kindergartners do not need to know how the Internet works. Knowing that devices are connected and can send things to each other is enough for this standard.
- You operate the device for any live demonstration, and the contact should be someone the student knows.
- This is a natural place to restate the privacy and technology rules from Lessons 9 and 10.
