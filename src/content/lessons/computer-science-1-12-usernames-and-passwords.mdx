**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **1.CYB.2** The student will discuss the importance of using a password to protect private information.
  - a. Describe the purpose of usernames and passwords.
  - b. Discuss how passwords are private information and are used to protect the privacy of information.

## Prep

- Put the paper slips in the box and close the lid.
- Write a pretend username on the name tag, such as a color and an animal (BlueTiger).

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Show the closed box. Say, "Inside are some things that belong only to me." Show the lock. Ask, "What does a lock do? Who should have the key?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Explain accounts: "Many apps and websites keep a space just for you. That space is called an account."
- Teach **username**: "A username is the name that tells the computer who you are." Put on the name tag. "It is like a name tag."
- Teach **password**: "A password is a secret word or code that proves it is really you. It is like the key."
- Show the purpose: the name tag says whose box it is, and the key opens it. "You need both. The username says who. The password proves it."
- Explain privacy: "A password is private information. It protects the other private information in your account, like your pictures, your messages, and your work."
- Ask, "If I gave my key to everyone, would my box still be safe?"
- State the rule: "Share passwords only with your trusted grown-ups. Not with friends, even best friends."

## Guided Practice (5 minutes)

1. Ask, "What is a username for? What is a password for?"
2. Act it out: the student is the computer guarding the box. You give the name tag and a wrong secret word. The student refuses. Then give the right one.
3. Ask, "What might happen if someone else knew your password?"
4. Sort three situations as safe or not safe: telling a parent your password; telling a friend your password; saying your password out loud at the library.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (3 minutes)

Ask the student three questions:

1. "What is the job of a username?"
2. "What is the job of a password?"
3. "Why do we keep passwords private, and who can know yours?"

Look for:
- The student describes the purpose of each.
- The student says a password is private and protects other information.
- The student names only trusted grown-ups.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Keep the lock and key in view and refer to them often.
- Practice the "computer guard" game several times.
- Focus on one message: passwords are secret.

**For students ready for more**
- Talk about what makes a password hard to guess: longer, and not your own name.
- Invent a practice passphrase from three unrelated words, for practice only.

## Extensions

- Make up a pretend username for a favorite toy.
- Notice when a grown-up signs in to something and name the username and the password.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Use pretend usernames and passwords only. Never use real ones in a lesson.
- A username should not be the student's full name. A made-up one models good practice.
- The standard asks students to discuss why passwords matter. Creating strong passwords comes in later grades.
