**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **7.DA.1** The student will utilize computational tools to visualize and evaluate data to draw conclusions and make predictions.
  - a. Develop computational models that simulate real-world phenomena, considering relevant variables and relationships.
  - b. Refine and modify computational models based on observed data and feedback, ensuring alignment with empirical evidence.

## Prep

- Choose a phenomenon with easily obtained data, such as a cooling cup of hot water measured every two minutes, a bouncing ball's heights, or a plant's growth.
- Collect the observed data in advance, or plan to collect it during the lesson.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "How do weather forecasters test whether their computer model is any good?"

## Direct Instruction (4 minutes)

- Define computational model: a program or spreadsheet that represents a real phenomenon with variables and rules, so it can be run to see what happens.
- Identify what to include: the relevant variables, such as starting temperature and room temperature, and the relationship between them, such as cooling faster when the difference is larger.
- Build a first version in which each step changes the value by a fixed amount.
- Explain refining: compare the model's output with observed data, adjust a rule or a value, and compare again. Empirical evidence means evidence from observation.

## Guided Practice (9 minutes)

1. The student lists the relevant variables and the relationship.
2. Build the first version of the model.
3. Enter the observed data beside the model's output.
4. Adjust one rule or value so that the model fits better, and note the change.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student makes a second refinement, records what was changed and why, and explains how the refined model aligns with the observed data.

Look for:
- Variables and relationships are relevant to the phenomenon.
- The model produces output that can be compared with data.
- Refinements are based on evidence and documented.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Use a prepared model and refine one value.
- Model something that changes by a constant amount.

**For students ready for more**
- Add a second variable that affects the outcome.
- Ask someone to critique the model and respond to the feedback.

## Extensions

- Collect a second set of data and check whether the model still fits.
- Explore a published simulation and identify its variables.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- A model is judged against evidence, not against how sophisticated it looks.
- Keep the model and data for Lesson 15.
