**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **4.DA.1** The student will identify the appropriate type of data needed to solve a problem or answer a question.
  - a. Analyze a problem to determine the appropriate type of data needed.
  - b. Evaluate the reliability of data sources.

## Prep

- Write four questions, such as: Which month is rainiest here? What snack should we bring for the team? How tall is the tallest mountain in Virginia? Is our street busier in the morning or evening?
- Write four source cards: a government weather website, a comment from a stranger online, a survey of the team, a count you do yourself.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "If you wanted to know which pizza topping your friends like best, would you measure the pizza or ask your friends?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Explain the first step in any data problem: decide what kind of data would answer it. Numbers that are measured or counted, categories or opinions, or facts that are looked up.
- Model with one question: the rainiest month needs rainfall measurements over many years.
- Define reliable: a source you can trust to be accurate.
- Give checks for reliability: Who collected it? Do they have expertise? How was it collected? Is it recent? Do other sources agree?

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. For each question, the student decides the type of data needed.
2. Match each question to the best source and explain.
3. Rank the four sources from most to least reliable for one question.
4. Ask, "When is your own count the most reliable source?"

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

Give a new problem: "Should the library open earlier on Saturdays?" The student states what data is needed, where to get it, and how reliable that source would be.

Look for:
- The data type fits the question.
- Reliability is judged using at least two checks.
- The student recognizes that a source can be reliable for one question and not another.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Work with two questions.
- Use the short check: who, how, when.

**For students ready for more**
- Find two sources that disagree and decide which to trust.
- Design a survey question that would give reliable data.

## Extensions

- Evaluate the source of a fact in a book or video.
- Ask a librarian how they judge sources.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Deciding what data is needed comes before collecting any.
- Reliability is a judgment, so ask for reasons.
