**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **7.AP.1** The student will apply computational thinking to design programs to accomplish a task as a means of creative expression or scientific exploration.
  - a. Identify patterns and repeated steps in an algorithm, problem, or process.
  - b. Decompose an algorithm, problem, or process into sub-components.
  - c. Abstract relevant information to identify essential details.

## Prep

- Help the student choose a direction: creative (an animated story, generative art, a music maker) or scientific (a simulation of population growth, falling objects, or a spreading rumor).

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "If you could make a program that expresses something or explores a question, what would it be?"

## Direct Instruction (4 minutes)

- State that the same thinking tools serve art and science.
- Decompose a sample project, a starry sky animation: draw one star, place it at random, repeat, and make stars twinkle.
- Identify patterns: every star uses the same steps with different values.
- Abstract: a star is described by only position, size, and brightness. Everything else is left out.
- Explain the result: a plan made of sub-components, a repeated procedure, and the essential data each needs.

## Guided Practice (7 minutes)

1. The student states the purpose of their project in one sentence.
2. Decompose it into sub-components on sticky notes.
3. Mark repeated steps that could become one procedure.
4. List the essential details and say what is deliberately left out.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student produces a one-page project brief: purpose, sub-components, repeated patterns, and the essential data, ready to plan as an algorithm.

Look for:
- Sub-components cover the project.
- Repeated steps are identified.
- Essential details are separated from the rest.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Use the starry sky example as the project.
- Decompose to one level.

**For students ready for more**
- Decompose to three levels and note which parts depend on others.
- Write two different abstractions of the same thing and say what each suits.

## Extensions

- Study how an artwork or a science simulation was built and identify its repeated procedures.
- Discuss the brief with someone and refine it.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Keep the brief. It feeds Lessons 3 through 7.
- Creative and scientific projects are both in the standard. Let the student choose.
