**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **4.CSY.2** The student will apply troubleshooting strategies when a computing system is not working as intended.
  - a. Identify hardware, software, and connectivity problems using accurate terminology.
  - b. Apply troubleshooting strategies to address hardware, software, and connectivity problems.

## Prep

- Write nine problem cards, three of each kind. Examples: the mouse does not move the pointer; an app closes by itself; a web page will not load but other apps work.
- Stage one safe connectivity problem, such as turning on airplane mode.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "A video will not play. Is the problem the device, the app, or the Internet? How could you tell?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Define the three categories. Hardware: a physical part. Software: a program or app. Connectivity: the link between the device and a network.
- Give signs of each. Connectivity: a no-connection message, slow loading, or other apps that need the Internet failing too.
- Add connectivity strategies to the checklist: check that Wi-Fi is on and airplane mode is off, check that you are on the right network, move closer to the router, and ask whether other devices are affected.
- Teach the narrowing question: "Does the problem happen in one app or everywhere?"

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. The student sorts the problem cards into the three categories and explains two.
2. Present the staged problem. The student describes it accurately and works through the checklist.
3. Ask, "What told you it was a connectivity problem?"
4. The student states what fixed it.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

Give three new problems aloud. The student names the category of each and the first strategy to apply, using accurate terms.

Look for:
- Problems are categorized correctly.
- The student uses accurate terms.
- Strategies fit the category.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Sort six cards.
- Keep a picture of each category on the table.

**For students ready for more**
- Give a problem that could be two categories and ask how to find out which.
- Write a troubleshooting flowchart that starts with the narrowing question.

## Extensions

- Draw a troubleshooting flowchart for the family.
- Ask an adult about a connectivity problem they solved.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Connectivity is the new category this year.
- Changing network settings stays with you. The student can check and report.
