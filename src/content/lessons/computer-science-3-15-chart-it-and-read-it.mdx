**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **3.DA.2** The student will create and evaluate data representations and conclusions.
  - a. Create charts and graphs based on data collection.
  - b. Analyze data to identify patterns, draw conclusions, and make predictions.

## Prep

- Check how to insert a chart from selected cells in your spreadsheet program.

## Warm-up (2 minutes)

Show the rows of numbers. Ask, "How long would it take to find the biggest value in a table with a thousand rows? What would help?"

## Direct Instruction (4 minutes)

- Explain that a chart turns rows of data into a picture where patterns stand out.
- Match chart types to purposes: a bar graph compares amounts, a line graph shows change over time, and a pictograph uses pictures.
- Model creating a chart: select the data, insert the chart, and add a title and axis labels.
- Model reading it: the highest and lowest, anything that repeats, and what might come next.

## Guided Practice (7 minutes)

1. The student selects the data and creates a chart.
2. The student adds a title and labels.
3. Ask, "What pattern do you see? What can you conclude?"
4. Ask for a prediction and what it is based on.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student draws a second chart of the same data by hand on grid paper, then writes one pattern, one conclusion, and one prediction.

Look for:
- The chart matches the data and has a title and labels.
- The pattern is visible in the chart.
- The prediction is tied to the data.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Make the chart by hand only.
- Provide the sentence starters: "I notice...", "So...", "I predict..."

**For students ready for more**
- Make two different chart types from the same data and say which shows it better.
- Add new data and see whether the prediction held.

## Extensions

- Chart the week's weather or the family's steps.
- Find a chart in the news and explain it to someone.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Making the same chart by hand and by computer shows what the computer is doing for us.
- Insist on titles and labels. A chart without them cannot be read by anyone else.
