**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **5.CYB.2** The student will explain how cybersecurity policies and laws are designed to protect individuals.
  - a. Explain the importance of policies and laws related to online use of computing devices and the Internet.
  - b. Research and discuss current cybersecurity policies and laws that protect individuals.
  - c. Explain legal consequences for inappropriate use of computing technologies.

## Prep

- Find and read a child-appropriate summary of at least one current law that protects people online, such as a children's online privacy law, and one policy, such as a website's terms of use or a school or library acceptable use policy. Confirm the details are current.
- Bookmark the pages.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "Why do we have traffic laws? What would roads be like without them?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Distinguish the two. A policy is a set of rules made by an organization for its own users, such as an acceptable use policy. A law is made by a government and applies to everyone it covers.
- Explain why they matter: they protect privacy and safety, set out what companies may do with people's information, and give people a way to get help when harmed.
- Introduce the law and the policy you prepared. Read the summaries together.
- Explain legal consequences in general terms: breaking a policy can mean losing access or an account. Breaking a law, such as breaking into accounts, stealing information, or serious harassment, can lead to fines or criminal charges, and for minors may involve parents, schools, and courts.

## Guided Practice (7 minutes)

1. The student takes notes on the law: who it protects and what it requires.
2. The student takes notes on the policy.
3. Ask, "How does this law protect someone your age?"
4. Discuss two scenarios and whether each breaks a policy, a law, or both.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student explains in their own words why online laws and policies are important, summarizes one law or policy researched, and gives one example of a legal consequence.

Look for:
- Policy and law are distinguished.
- The summary is accurate to the source.
- The consequence fits the action.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Research the policy only and discuss the law together.
- Read the source aloud.

**For students ready for more**
- Compare an age rule in a website's terms with what the law requires.
- Find out who to contact when someone's rights online are violated.

## Extensions

- Read the terms summary of a service the family uses.
- Update the family technology rules based on what was learned.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Laws change. Use current sources and check them before teaching.
- Stay with summaries from official or educational sources.
