**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **1.DA.2** The student will create representations of data to make predictions and draw conclusions.
  - c. Identify patterns and describe trends in data visualizations of various formats.
  - d. Use data to answer questions, draw conclusions, and make predictions.

## Prep

- Make a weather tally table for one or two weeks with sunny, cloudy, and rainy rows.
- Make a graph that goes up over several days, such as books read on Monday through Friday (1, 2, 2, 3, 4).

## Warm-up (2 minutes)

Lay out last lesson's graph. Ask, "What is the first thing you notice?" Accept any true observation.

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Explain: "Graphs and tables are data visualizations. They let us see data. When we read them, we look for patterns."
- Teach pattern words with the picture graph: most, fewest, the same, more than, fewer than.
- Show the graph that changes over days. "When data keeps going up, keeps going down, or stays the same over time, we call that a trend." Trace the tops of the columns with your finger. "This trend is going up."
- Model four kinds of thinking:
  - Answer a question: "How many books on Wednesday?"
  - Describe a trend: "The number of books went up during the week."
  - Draw a conclusion: "So this reader read more at the end of the week than at the start."
  - Make a prediction: "If the trend keeps going, I predict five books on Saturday."
- Say, "A prediction is a smart guess based on the data. It might not come true."

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. Show the weather table. Ask, "Which kind of weather happened most? Fewest?"
2. Ask, "What pattern do you see?"
3. Ask, "What can we say for sure about these two weeks?"
4. Ask, "What kind of weather would you predict for tomorrow? What in the data makes you think so?"
5. Compare the table and a graph. Ask, "Which one is easier to read for this question?"

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

Return to the student's own graph from Lesson 14. The student tells you one pattern, answers one question you ask, states one conclusion, and makes one prediction with a reason.

Look for:
- The student uses comparison words accurately.
- The conclusion is supported by the data.
- The prediction comes with a reason from the data.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Stay with most and fewest.
- Use one visualization for the whole lesson.
- Offer two possible conclusions and ask which one the data supports.

**For students ready for more**
- Ask "how many more" and "how many in all" questions.
- Ask what new data would change the prediction.

## Extensions

- Check the weather prediction tomorrow and talk about why it did or did not come true.
- Find a simple graph in a children's magazine or on a cereal box and read it together.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- The standard says "visualizations of various formats," so use at least two: a table and a graph.
- A trend needs data over time. That is why the second prepared graph shows days in order.
- Ask "How do you know?" often. Pointing back to the data is the habit to build.
