**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **6.IC.2** The student will analyze the impact of screen time on physical and mental health.
  - a. Analyze and describe the impact of excessive technology usage may have on one’s physical health.
  - b. Examine the impact of blue light on sleep patterns and regulations.
  - c. Propose strategies that provide alternatives of technology usage to promote physical activity.

## Prep

- If possible, have the student log evening screen use, bedtime, and how rested they felt for three days beforehand.
- Read a current, reliable summary on screens and sleep from a health organization so that what you say is accurate.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "How does your body feel after two hours on a screen without moving? Where do you notice it?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Describe effects of excessive use on physical health: eye strain, headaches, neck and back strain from posture, less physical activity, and disrupted sleep.
- Explain blue light: screens give off light that is rich in blue wavelengths. Research indicates that bright light in the evening, blue light in particular, can delay the body's release of melatonin, the hormone that signals sleep, and shift the body's daily clock.
- Give sleep strategies: stop screens well before bed, use night or dimming settings, and keep devices out of the bedroom.
- Explain proposing alternatives: activities that replace sitting screen time with movement, and uses of technology that support movement.

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. Look at the log, or estimate. What pattern appears between evening screens and sleep?
2. The student lists physical effects they have noticed.
3. Brainstorm ten alternatives that involve physical activity.
4. Choose three and plan when they would replace screen time.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student explains two physical effects of excessive use, describes how blue light can affect sleep, and proposes a realistic one-week plan with three active alternatives.

Look for:
- Physical effects are accurate.
- The blue light explanation mentions melatonin or the body's clock and sleep timing.
- The plan is specific and realistic.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Focus on posture and eyes with a quick demonstration.
- Plan one alternative.

**For students ready for more**
- Analyze a week of personal data and present it in a chart.
- Compare advice from two health sources.

## Extensions

- Try the plan for a week and review the log.
- Set up night settings on family devices with an adult.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Health guidance changes. Check a current source.
- Avoid alarm. The aim is informed habits.
