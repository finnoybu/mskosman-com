**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **7.CYB.1** The student will differentiate physical and digital security measures that protect electronic information.
  - a. Compare and contrast physical and digital security measures.
  - b. Research and synthesize the tradeoffs between usability and security.

## Prep

- Write twelve cards, such as a locked server room, a password, a security camera, encryption, a cable lock, two-step sign-in, a badge reader, a firewall, a privacy screen, antivirus software, a shredder, automatic screen lock.
- Bookmark a short article on balancing security and convenience.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "Which is safer: a door with ten locks or a door with one? Which would you rather use every day?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Define physical security measures: protections for the hardware and places where information is kept.
- Define digital security measures: protections within software and networks.
- Compare them: both control who gets access, both can fail, and each is needed because either can be bypassed through the other. A strong password does not help if the device is stolen while unlocked.
- Introduce the tradeoff: more security often means more steps, time, or restrictions. If security is too hard to use, people work around it, which lowers security.

## Guided Practice (7 minutes)

1. The student sorts the cards and finds one that could be both.
2. Pair each physical measure with a digital one that protects the same information.
3. Read the article and note two examples of the tradeoff.
4. Chart three measures: security gained and usability lost.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student compares one physical and one digital measure, and synthesizes the research into a recommendation for how much security a family photo account should have.

Look for:
- Similarities and differences are stated.
- The tradeoff is explained with evidence from the source.
- The recommendation balances both sides.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Sort eight cards.
- Chart one measure.

**For students ready for more**
- Design layered security for a small office and justify each layer.
- Explain why a rule requiring very frequent password changes can backfire.

## Extensions

- Audit the physical and digital security of a family device.
- Notice a security measure that annoys you and work out what it protects.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Layers matter: physical and digital measures cover each other's gaps.
- Security that people avoid using fails. That is the usability lesson.
