**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **4.AP.1** The student will apply computational thinking to identify patterns and design algorithms to compare and contrast multiple algorithms used for the same task.
  - c. Describe patterns within multiple algorithms.
  - d. Determine which algorithm is most effective for a given task.

## Prep

- Draw a comparison chart with rows for each algorithm and columns: steps, time, easy to follow, works every time.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "If two recipes both make good pancakes, how would you choose between them?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Define effective: an algorithm is effective when it gets the correct result, and more effective when it does so with less time, fewer steps, or less chance of error.
- Look for patterns across the two sorting algorithms: both compare two cards at a time, both repeat, and both have a stopping condition.
- Explain that the best choice depends on the situation: few items or many, already nearly sorted or not, done by a person or a computer.
- Model filling in one row of the chart.

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. The student lists three patterns the algorithms share and one way they differ.
2. Run each algorithm with 5 cards and with 10 cards. Record time and number of swaps.
3. Fill in the chart together.
4. Ask, "Which is more effective for 5 cards? For 100? Why?"

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (5 minutes)

Present two algorithms for finding a word in a dictionary: start at page one and check every page, or open in the middle and decide which half to search. The student names a shared pattern and decides which is more effective, with a reason.

Look for:
- Shared patterns such as repetition and comparison are identified.
- The choice is supported by evidence such as steps or time.
- The student recognizes that the situation affects the choice.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Compare on one measure only: time.
- Use very different algorithms so the contrast is clear.

**For students ready for more**
- Predict how the number of steps grows as the list doubles.
- Find a situation where the slower algorithm is the better choice.

## Extensions

- Time two ways of doing a chore and decide which is more effective.
- Compare two search strategies for a lost object.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- "Most effective" depends on the task. Push for a reason, not just a winner.
- This is the student's first look at efficiency, a theme in later grades.
