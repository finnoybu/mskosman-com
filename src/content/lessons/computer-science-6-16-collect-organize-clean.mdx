**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **6.DA.1** The student will utilize computational tools to collect and organize data.
  - a. Select and use appropriate computational tools to collect data.
  - b. Organize data to make it easier to understand and use.
  - c. Clean data to remove and correct errors.
  - d. Analyze data sources for accuracy and reliability.

## Prep

- Choose either a small public data set from an official source, such as weather records, or set up a short survey form that feeds a spreadsheet.
- Download or collect about 20 rows before the lesson.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "If you needed the daily temperature for the last month, would you measure it yourself, ask people, or find a record? What decides it?"

## Direct Instruction (4 minutes)

- Describe computational tools for collecting data: online forms and surveys, sensors and apps that log data, spreadsheets for manual entry, and downloadable data sets.
- Explain selecting a tool: it depends on whether the data already exists, how much is needed, and how accurate it must be.
- Review organizing: clear headings, one record per row, consistent units and formats, and sorting or filtering.
- Review cleaning: remove duplicates, correct obvious errors, standardize formats, and handle missing values openly.
- Review analyzing the source: who produced it, how, when, and whether it can be checked.

## Guided Practice (9 minutes)

1. The student explains which tool fits the question and why.
2. Open the data in the spreadsheet and organize it.
3. The student finds and cleans at least three problems, keeping a note of each change.
4. Together, evaluate the source for accuracy and reliability.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student writes a short data note: the tool used and why, how the data was organized, what was cleaned, and a judgment of the source's reliability.

Look for:
- The tool fits the kind of data.
- Cleaning changes are documented.
- The reliability judgment uses specific evidence.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Use a prepared data set of ten rows with two planted errors.
- Organize together and let the student clean.

**For students ready for more**
- Use filters to answer a question about a subset.
- Compare the data with a second source and report differences.

## Extensions

- Run a short survey of family or friends with a form tool.
- Keep the data note with the file as documentation.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Documenting cleaning protects honesty. Readers should know what was changed.
- Keep the cleaned file for Lessons 18 and 19.
