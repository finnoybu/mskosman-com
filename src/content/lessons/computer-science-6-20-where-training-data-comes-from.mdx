**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **6.DA.4** The student will identify ways people curate and provide training data.
  - a. Identify and list ways people provide data that is used as training data.
  - b. Discuss the role of human intervention in curating training data.
  - c. Identify and describe the effect training data has on the accuracy of artificial intelligence systems.

## Prep

- Prepare 20 labeled cards for two categories, with three wrong labels, two duplicates, and two that belong to neither category.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "When you tag a friend in a photo or click 'not interested' on a video, who or what learns from that?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- List ways people provide data that becomes training data: posting text and photos, tagging and labeling, rating and reviewing, clicking and searching, using voice assistants, completing image puzzles, and being recorded by sensors and devices.
- Note that people often do not realize they are providing it.
- Define curating: people select, clean, label, and check training data. They decide what to include, fix wrong labels, remove harmful or irrelevant items, and check for balance.
- Explain the effect on accuracy: accurate, well-labeled, representative data leads to more accurate systems. Errors, gaps, and imbalance lead to mistakes.

## Guided Practice (7 minutes)

1. The student acts as the curator of the card set: find and fix wrong labels, remove duplicates and irrelevant items.
2. Ask, "What decisions did you make? Could two curators decide differently?"
3. Compare a system trained on the original set with one trained on the curated set.
4. List three ways the student's own activity might become training data.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student lists four ways people provide training data, explains what curating involves and why people are needed for it, and describes how data quality affects accuracy.

Look for:
- Sources of training data are realistic.
- Human decisions in curation are identified.
- Data quality is linked to accuracy.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Curate a set of ten cards with two problems.
- Focus on labeling.

**For students ready for more**
- Discuss whether people should be asked before their data is used for training.
- Explain how a curator's own assumptions could introduce bias.

## Extensions

- Notice three moments in a day when you may be providing data.
- Read with an adult how one service says it uses customer data.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- This connects to algorithmic bias in Lesson 13.
- Curation is human judgment, which is why it matters who does it.
