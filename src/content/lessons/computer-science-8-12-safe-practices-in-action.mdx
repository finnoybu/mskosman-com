**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **8.CYB.1** The student will investigate and describe ways to protect sensitive data from malware and other attacks.
  - c. Compare and contrast safe and unsafe computing practices.
  - e. Model common prevention practices for cyber-attacks.

## Prep

- Write paired cards showing an unsafe and a safe version of the same practice, such as reusing one password and using unique passwords; clicking a link in an unexpected message and going to the site directly; ignoring updates and installing them; using public Wi-Fi for banking and waiting for a trusted network; no backups and regular backups.
- Prepare a checklist of prevention practices.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "Which is more likely to protect you: expensive security software or your own habits? Why?"

## Direct Instruction (4 minutes)

- Compare each pair: what makes one safe and the other unsafe, and which attack the safe version prevents.
- Group prevention practices: protect accounts (unique passwords, two-step sign-in), protect devices (updates, screen lock, security software), protect data (backups, encryption), and protect yourself (verify senders, think before clicking, limit sharing).
- Define modeling a practice: doing it correctly yourself and showing others how.

## Guided Practice (8 minutes)

1. The student sorts the cards and matches each safe practice to the attack it prevents.
2. On the device, the student models checking for updates and reviewing a sign-in setting, with you present.
3. The student models examining a suspicious message on paper: sender, link, urgency.
4. Complete the checklist for one family device.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student teaches you three prevention practices as if you were new to them, demonstrating each, and explains what could happen without them.

Look for:
- Safe and unsafe practices are compared with reasons.
- Practices are demonstrated correctly.
- Each is linked to the attack it prevents.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Compare three pairs.
- Model one practice.

**For students ready for more**
- Create a short training session for younger students or relatives.
- Set up a backup routine with an adult.

## Extensions

- Run the checklist on each family device.
- Teach a grandparent to recognize a suspicious message.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- The standard says "model." The student should perform the practices, not only list them.
- Changes to settings and accounts are made with you present.
