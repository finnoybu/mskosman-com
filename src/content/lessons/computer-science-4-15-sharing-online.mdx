**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **4.CYB.3** The student will examine how information is shared online and explain the importance of cybersecurity.
  - a. Investigate multiple ways people share information online.
  - b. Determine and describe when information should be shared and to whom it should be shared.
  - c. Describe how personal information can be collected and shared online.
  - d. Explain the importance of cybersecurity.

## Prep

- Make the cards.
- Think about which of these your family uses.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "When you send a message, where does it go? Who else could see it?"

## Direct Instruction (6 minutes)

- Go through the ways people share online and who can usually see each: one person, a group, or anyone.
- Give a rule for when and to whom: share private information only when it is needed, only with people or organizations you and a trusted adult know, and only through a way that reaches just them.
- Explain collection. Some information you give directly in forms and profiles. Some is collected as you use a service, such as what you search for, where the device is, and what you click.
- Explain how it gets shared further: others can forward or screenshot it, and companies may share it with other companies.
- State why cybersecurity matters: it protects people's information, money, and safety, and keeps the services everyone depends on working.

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. The student fills in the chart for four ways of sharing.
2. Give three situations. The student decides whether to share, with whom, and how.
3. Ask, "What might an app learn about you without you typing it?"
4. The student completes: "Cybersecurity is important because..."

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student explains to you which way they would use to send an address to a relative and why, describes one way information is collected without being typed, and gives two reasons cybersecurity matters.

Look for:
- The method chosen fits the sensitivity of the information.
- Collection is described as both direct and through use.
- Reasons relate to protecting people.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Compare two ways only: a private message and a public post.
- Use real family examples.

**For students ready for more**
- Read the short summary of an app's privacy practices with an adult.
- Explain why a screenshot makes "deleting" unreliable.

## Extensions

- Ask family members which ways they use to share different kinds of information.
- Make a chart for the family: what goes where.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Keep the tone factual. Collection is how many services work, and knowing it lets people choose.
- This lesson ties the cybersecurity strand together for the year.
