**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **8.NI.1** The student will model and describe the role of computing devices in transmitting data in and on computing networks and the Internet.
  - a. Identify the roles of computing devices: routers, switches, servers, and clients communicating over a network.
  - b. Design a network topology of computing devices.

## Prep

- Write a scenario, such as a small office with eight computers, two printers, a file server, Wi-Fi for visitors, and an Internet connection.
- Make device cards: clients, server, switch, router, wireless access point, printer.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "In your home, what is the one box that everything connects to? What do you think it is actually doing?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Define the roles. Client: a device that requests services or data. Server: a computer that provides services or data to clients. Switch: connects devices within one network and sends data only to the device it is meant for. Router: connects different networks and forwards data between them, such as between a local network and the Internet. Note that a home router usually combines a router, a switch, and a wireless access point.
- Define topology: the arrangement of devices and connections.
- Show common topologies. Star: every device connects to a central switch. Bus: devices share one line. Ring: devices connect in a loop. Mesh: devices have multiple connections to one another.
- Compare them briefly on cost, reliability, and ease of adding devices.

## Guided Practice (8 minutes)

1. The student lays out the device cards for the scenario.
2. Connect them with yarn or lines, choosing a topology.
3. Label each device's role.
4. Ask, "What happens if this device fails? Where is the single point of failure?"

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student draws the final topology on paper with labels, explains the role of each device, and justifies the design.

Look for:
- Roles are identified correctly.
- The topology connects every device and reaches the Internet.
- The design is justified, including its weak points.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Design a home network with five devices.
- Use a star topology.

**For students ready for more**
- Add redundancy so that no single failure disconnects everything.
- Separate visitor Wi-Fi from the office devices and explain why.

## Extensions

- Map your home network.
- Research which topology large data centers use.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Students often call everything a router. Distinguishing a switch from a router is the key step.
- Asking what happens when something fails turns a drawing into a design.
