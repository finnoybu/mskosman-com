**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **3.DA.3** The student will create models that can represent a physical object or process.
  - a. Create a model to represent a physical object or process.
  - b. Identify how computing devices are used to create models.
  - c. Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of using computing devices to create models.

## Prep

- Choose something to model, such as the water cycle, a plant's growth, or the layout of a room.
- Find one computer model to show: an animated weather forecast map, a map app's 3D view, or a simple simulation.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Hold up a toy car or a globe. Ask, "How is this like the real thing? How is it different? Why is it useful anyway?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Define: "A model is a simpler version of a real object or process that helps us understand it." A model leaves out details on purpose.
- Give examples of both kinds: a globe models an object, and a diagram of the water cycle models a process.
- Show the computer model. Explain that computers build models from data: weather forecasts, maps, building designs, and game worlds.
- Discuss advantages of computer models: fast to change, can show things too big, small, slow, or dangerous to see, and easy to share. Disadvantages: need a device and power, can be wrong if the data is wrong, and you cannot touch them.

## Guided Practice (8 minutes)

1. The student plans a model: what it represents and which details to keep or leave out.
2. The student builds it from craft materials or as a labeled drawing.
3. The student explains how the model matches the real thing.
4. Compare with the computer model. Ask, "What can each do that the other cannot?"

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student presents their model, names two ways computers are used to make models, and gives one advantage and one disadvantage of computer models.

Look for:
- The model represents a real object or process and the student can explain it.
- The student identifies real uses of computer models.
- Both an advantage and a disadvantage are given.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Model an object, which is easier than a process.
- Offer a choice of two things to model.

**For students ready for more**
- Make a second model of the same thing in a different form and compare.
- Build a simple model in a drawing or block-building app.

## Extensions

- Find three models at home: a map, a toy, a diagram in a manual.
- Watch a weather forecast and discuss what data the model uses.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- The physical model satisfies the first part of the standard. A device is needed only to view an example.
- Link back to Lesson 14: a model built from bad data gives bad answers.
