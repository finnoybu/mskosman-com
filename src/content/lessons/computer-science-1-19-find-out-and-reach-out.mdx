**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **1.NI.1** The student will explain that computing devices and the use of the Internet allow people the ability to gather information and connect with others.
  - a. Describe how the Internet can be used to gather information.
  - b. Explain ways people communicate using computing devices and the Internet.
- **1.IC.1** The student will describe how computing technologies impact daily tasks and communication.
  - b. Describe how computing devices are used in communication.

## Prep

- Choose a child-friendly website or search tool you trust and open it before the lesson.
- Have one question ready in case the student does not think of one, such as how long an elephant lives.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "What is something you wonder about?" Write the question down. Ask, "Where could we look to find the answer?" List the ideas: ask someone, look in a book, look on the Internet.

## Direct Instruction (6 minutes)

- Review: "The Internet connects computing devices all around the world."
- Explain gathering information: "People put information on the Internet, such as facts, pictures, videos, and maps. When our device is connected, we can look for it. Gathering information means finding and collecting what we want to know."
- Model: with the student watching, type the question into the tool you chose. Read one result aloud. Say what you found.
- Add a caution: "Not everything on the Internet is true. We look with a grown-up and choose places we trust."
- Compare with the book: "A book has what is printed in it. The Internet can have newer information and much more of it, but we have to choose carefully."
- Explain communicating: "The Internet also lets people connect with each other." Name ways: video calls, messages, email, sharing photos, and learning together in an online class.
- Explain how: "My device sends the message through the Internet to the other person's device."

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. Together, gather the answer to a second question. The student tells you what to type and helps choose what to read.
2. Ask, "How did the Internet help us gather information?"
3. Ask, "What are three ways our family communicates using devices and the Internet?" The student draws one.
4. Ask, "Why is it useful to be able to do that?"

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

Ask the student to explain, as if to a younger child:

1. "How can the Internet help you find something out?"
2. "What are two ways people communicate using computing devices and the Internet?"

Look for:
- The student describes looking up information as one use of the Internet.
- The student explains at least two ways of communicating.
- The student mentions doing this with a trusted grown-up.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Use one simple question with a clear answer.
- Focus on one way of communicating the student has experienced.
- Let the student point and choose while you type and read.

**For students ready for more**
- Look up the same question in a book and online and compare the answers.
- Talk about why two websites might say different things.

## Extensions

- Start a wonder list. Once a week, gather the answer to one question together.
- With a grown-up, send a photo of a drawing to a relative and ask for a reply.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- You operate the device and choose the sites. The student directs the search and discusses what is found.
- This is the last lesson in the set and connects back to safe communication in Lesson 11.
- The standard has two parts, gathering information and connecting with others. Make sure the student can talk about both.
