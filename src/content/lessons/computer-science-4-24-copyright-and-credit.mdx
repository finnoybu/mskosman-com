**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **4.IC.4** The student will describe the importance of copyrights and intellectual property rights.
  - a. Demonstrate an understanding of copyright and the fair use of information.
  - b. Explain how intellectual property can be protected.
  - c. Give proper attribution to the original author of digital and online content.

## Prep

- Choose an online article or image with a visible author or creator and date.
- Write an attribution template: Title, Author or Creator, Website or Source, Date, Web address.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "If you wrote a song and someone else sold it, what would you want to be able to do?"

## Direct Instruction (6 minutes)

- Define intellectual property: creations of the mind, such as stories, music, art, inventions, software, and brand names.
- Review copyright: the creator decides how the work is copied and used.
- Introduce fair use: the law allows limited use of copyrighted work without permission for purposes such as teaching, reporting, or commenting, especially when only a small part is used and it does not replace the original. Fair use depends on the situation.
- Explain protections: copyright for creative work, patents for inventions, trademarks for names and logos, and licenses that state what others may do.
- Define attribution: giving credit by naming the creator and source. Model with the template.

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. Decide whether each of four uses is likely fair: quoting two sentences in a report with credit; copying a whole book for friends; showing a short clip to discuss it in a lesson; using a logo on things you sell.
2. Match three creations with their protection.
3. The student writes a full attribution for the chosen article or image.
4. Ask, "Does giving credit mean you have permission?" (No. They are separate.)

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student explains copyright and fair use in their own words, names two ways intellectual property is protected, and writes a correct attribution for a second source.

Look for:
- Fair use is described as limited and dependent on purpose and amount.
- Protections are matched to the right kinds of work.
- The attribution includes creator, title, and source.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Focus on copyright and attribution.
- Fill in the template together.

**For students ready for more**
- Find an image with a license that allows reuse and explain what the license permits.
- Add a sources list to a past project.

## Extensions

- Add attributions to a presentation or report.
- Put a copyright notice on your own creative work.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Fair use is a judgment made case by case. Avoid giving it as a simple rule.
- Credit and permission are different things. Make that distinction clear.
