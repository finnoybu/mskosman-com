**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **5.CSY.1** The student will explain how computing systems are used to collect and exchange data.
  - a. Identify and explain how computing systems store data representations, including images and sound.
  - b. Describe the role of processing speed and storage capacity when collecting and exchanging data.

## Prep

- Prepare a small picture, such as a heart or a letter, and an 8 by 8 grid and a 16 by 16 grid.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Zoom in on a photo on a screen until the squares show, or describe it. Ask, "What is a picture made of?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Images: a picture is a grid of tiny squares called pixels. Each pixel's color is stored as numbers, such as an RGB value. More pixels give more detail and a bigger file.
- Sound: a microphone measures the sound wave thousands of times each second. Each measurement, called a sample, is stored as a number. More samples give better quality and a bigger file.
- Storage capacity: how much data a device can hold, measured in bytes, with kilobytes, megabytes, and gigabytes as larger units.
- Processing speed: how quickly the processor can work on data. Large files take longer to save, open, and send, and a slow processor or small storage limits what can be collected and exchanged.

## Guided Practice (7 minutes)

1. The student draws the picture on the 8 by 8 grid by filling squares.
2. Draw it again on the 16 by 16 grid and compare detail.
3. Count the squares in each grid. Ask, "Which needs more storage? Why?"
4. If a device is available, compare the file sizes of a photo, a song, and a video.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student explains how an image and a sound are stored, then answers: "Why does a video take longer to send than a text message, and why might an older tablet struggle with it?"

Look for:
- Images are described as pixels with numeric color values.
- Sound is described as many numeric samples.
- The answer connects file size with storage capacity and processing speed.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Do the image part only and discuss sound briefly.
- Use black and white pixels written as 1 and 0.

**For students ready for more**
- Write the 8 by 8 picture as rows of 1s and 0s and have someone redraw it from the numbers.
- Estimate how many photos fit on a device from its capacity and a photo's size.

## Extensions

- Make pixel art and send its number code to a friend.
- Check how much storage is free on a family device.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- This builds on binary and RGB from earlier grades.
- Exact byte arithmetic is not needed. The relationship between detail, size, speed, and capacity is the goal.
