**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **4.NI.1** The student will identify the interrelationship between computing devices and a computing network.
  - a. Define client and server.
  - b. Describe how packets are used to transmit information on a network.
  - e. Model how computing devices in a network transmit and receive information.

## Prep

- Write a message of about twelve words on a strip of paper.
- Set up stations around the room: a client, a server, and two or three routers between them.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "When you open a website, where is the page before it appears on your screen?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Define client: a device that requests information or a service. Your tablet is a client.
- Define server: a computer that stores information and sends it when asked.
- Define packets: information is broken into small pieces. Each packet carries part of the data, the address it is going to, the address it came from, and its number in the sequence.
- Explain the trip: packets travel through routers, may take different paths, can arrive out of order, and are put back together by number. Missing packets are requested again.

## Guided Practice (8 minutes)

1. The client writes a request ("send me the message") and passes it through the routers to the server.
2. At the server, cut the message into six pieces. Number each, put it in an envelope, and address it to the client.
3. Send envelopes by different routes. Hold one back so that it arrives late.
4. The client opens the envelopes, reorders the pieces by number, and reads the message.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student repeats the model with a new message while narrating each step, then defines client, server, and packet.

Look for:
- Client and server roles are correct.
- Packets carry addresses and numbers.
- The student explains reassembly and what happens when a packet is missing.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Use three packets and one router.
- Keep definition cards at each station.

**For students ready for more**
- Lose a packet on purpose and act out the request to resend it.
- Explain why packets are better than sending one large piece.

## Extensions

- Draw a comic strip of a packet's journey.
- Identify which devices at home act as clients.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- The envelope activity is the model the standard asks for.
- Addresses here are pretend. There is no need to introduce real address formats.
