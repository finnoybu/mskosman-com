**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **1.AP.2** The student will plan and implement algorithms that include the use of sequence and an event based on a predetermined task.
  - b. Construct step-by-step instructions that include decision-making and repetition.

## Prep

- Write REPEAT on one card and IF on another, each with a simple picture (a loop arrow for repeat, a fork in a road for if).

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Play a quick game: "If I touch my nose, you jump. If I touch my ear, you sit." Try it several times. Then say, "Hop three times." Ask, "Did I have to say hop, hop, hop?"

## Direct Instruction (6 minutes)

- Review: "An algorithm is a set of step-by-step instructions."
- Teach repetition: "When a step happens more than once, we can say repeat. Repeat means do it again." Show the REPEAT card. Example: "Repeat 3 times: clap."
- Teach decision-making: "Sometimes the next step depends on something. We use the word if." Show the IF card. Example: "If it is raining, take an umbrella."
- Model an algorithm for sorting the bag of blocks, laying out cards as you speak:
  1. Take one block out of the bag.
  2. IF it is red, put it in the left bowl. IF it is blue, put it in the right bowl.
  3. REPEAT until the bag is empty.
- Follow your own instructions exactly so the student sees the decision and the repeat at work.

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. Together, build instructions for feeding a pretend pet: scoop food, pour it in the bowl, repeat two times, IF the water bowl is empty, fill it.
2. The student places the REPEAT and IF cards where they belong.
3. Act it out and check that it works.
4. Ask, "Which step was a decision? Which step repeated?"

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student constructs their own short set of instructions that uses IF once and REPEAT once. They may say it, draw it, or lay out cards. You follow the instructions exactly.

Look for:
- The steps are in a workable order.
- One step is a decision that uses "if."
- One step repeats.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Teach repetition in one session and decisions in another.
- Use whole-body examples: "Repeat 4 times: jump."
- Provide a partly built set of instructions with one blank for the student to fill in.

**For students ready for more**
- Use "if" with "otherwise": "If the light is green, go. Otherwise, wait."
- Put a decision inside a repeated step.

## Extensions

- Find the repeats in a song with a chorus or in a recipe ("stir 10 times").
- Find "if" rules at home: "If your hands are dirty, wash them."

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- "Decision-making and repetition" are the standard's words. In later grades these are called conditionals and loops. Those terms are not needed yet.
- Following the instructions literally shows the student whether the repeat and the decision are clear.
- Keep the IF and REPEAT cards for Lessons 5 and 6.
