**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **7.IC.2** The student will describe and explain the impact of screen time on interactions with others.
  - a. Describe the positive and negative impact of social media on socialization.
  - c. Describe and explain the evolution of screen time and the impact it has had on social interactions.

## Prep

- Arrange a five-minute interview with a parent, grandparent, or other adult about how they spent time with friends at age twelve.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "How do you and your friends make plans? How do you think your grandparents did at your age?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Define socialization: how people learn to interact, build relationships, and take part in groups.
- Trace the evolution of screen time: shared television, home computers, the Internet, mobile phones, smartphones, and social media. Screens moved from shared and fixed to personal and constant.
- Describe effects on interaction: more contact across distance and more ways to find people with shared interests; also less undivided attention, fewer unplanned in-person encounters, and conversations happening in public view.
- Chart social media specifically. Positives: staying connected, community, support, self-expression. Negatives: comparison, exclusion, conflict that spreads, and pressure to be always available.

## Guided Practice (7 minutes)

1. The student interviews the adult and takes notes.
2. Build the timeline, adding the interview details.
3. Complete the positives and negatives chart with the student's own examples.
4. Ask, "What has been gained? What has been lost?"

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student describes two positive and two negative effects of social media on socialization and explains, using the timeline, how the evolution of screen time changed the way people interact.

Look for:
- Effects on socialization are specific.
- The evolution is described in stages.
- Changes in interaction are linked to changes in screens.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Build a three-stage timeline.
- Chart one positive and one negative.

**For students ready for more**
- Interview people from three generations and compare.
- Analyze how one platform's features shape how people interact.

## Extensions

- Try an older way of making plans for a week.
- Discuss the interview findings as a family.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Many seventh graders use social media or soon will. Speak to their real experience.
- Present both sides. The standard asks for positive and negative impact.
