**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **8.AP.1** The student will apply computational thinking to construct programs to accomplish a task as a means of creative expression or scientific exploration.
  - d. Use pseudocode, decision trees, or flowcharts to illustrate complex problems as algorithms.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Show a paragraph describing a complicated rule, such as how a game decides the winner of a round. Ask, "Could you program this from the paragraph alone? What is unclear?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Compare the three tools. Pseudocode suits step-by-step logic with loops and functions. A decision tree suits a choice with many branching conditions. A flowchart suits a process where the path depends on conditions and loops back.
- Set conventions: consistent indentation and keywords in pseudocode; one question per node in a tree; standard symbols and labeled arrows in a flowchart.
- Model one complex rule in two forms and compare which is clearer.
- Explain levels of detail: a top-level diagram of the whole program and a detailed one for each difficult part.

## Guided Practice (8 minutes)

1. The student chooses the most complex part of their project.
2. Illustrate it with the tool that fits best.
3. Trace it with two test cases to check it.
4. Illustrate the top-level flow of the whole program in a second form.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student delivers two illustrations, one top-level and one detailed, and explains why each form was chosen.

Look for:
- The algorithm is complete, with every path leading somewhere.
- Conventions are followed.
- The choice of form is justified.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Illustrate a moderately complex rule in pseudocode.
- Provide a partly completed flowchart.

**For students ready for more**
- Illustrate the same algorithm in all three forms and compare.
- Have someone else implement a part from the illustration alone.

## Extensions

- Illustrate a real-world process, such as how a refund request is handled.
- Find a published flowchart and critique it.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- If the illustration cannot be traced, the algorithm is not yet understood.
- Different parts of one program may be best shown in different forms.
