**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **K.CYB.2** The student will describe the importance of protecting personal information online.
  - a. Describe what personal information should be shared and not shared.
  - b. Determine to whom personal information should be shared.
  - c. Identify safe practices to keep personal information private.

## Prep

- Make the information cards, one item per card, with a small drawing on each.
- Draw a closed lock on one sheet of paper and an open lock on another.
- Decide who the student's trusted grown-ups are.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "What are some things that are special to you and belong only to you?" Then ask, "Would you give your house key to someone you do not know? Why not?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Define: "Personal information is information that is about you, like your full name, where you live, your phone number, your birthday, and your passwords."
- Explain why it matters: "Personal information can tell a stranger who you are and where to find you. So we keep it private."
- Show the closed lock sheet (private) and the open lock sheet (okay to share). Place two example cards: home address on the closed lock, favorite color on the open lock. Say why for each.
- Teach who: "Personal information is shared only with trusted grown-ups, like a parent or the people a parent says are okay. Online, we do not share it with anyone unless a trusted grown-up says yes."
- Teach three safe practices: ask a grown-up before typing anything about yourself; keep passwords secret from everyone except your trusted grown-ups; if someone online asks about you, stop and tell.

## Guided Practice (5 minutes)

1. Turn over one card at a time. The student decides: closed lock or open lock?
2. Ask, "Why?" for each card.
3. For two private cards, ask, "Is there anyone you could share this with?" Guide the student to name trusted grown-ups.
4. Role-play: "A game asks you to type your address. What do you do?"

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

Shuffle the cards. The student sorts them alone onto the two sheets. Then ask three questions:

1. "Tell me one thing you should not share online."
2. "Who is it okay to share personal information with?"
3. "What is one way to keep your information private?"

Look for:
- Name, address, phone number, birthday, and password are placed on the closed lock.
- The student names trusted grown-ups as the people to share with.
- The student states at least one safe practice.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Use four cards: two clearly private, two clearly okay.
- Use pictures on every card.
- Repeat the single rule, "Ask first," as the main takeaway.

**For students ready for more**
- Discuss in-between cases, such as a first name only or a photo, and why a grown-up should decide.
- The student makes new cards for you to sort.

## Extensions

- Make up a silly practice password together and talk about why real ones stay secret.
- Reread the family technology rules and add a privacy rule if one is missing.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Decide ahead of time who counts as a trusted grown-up for this student, and name those people clearly.
- Keep the tone calm and practical. The aim is a habit of asking first, not fear of the internet.
- Many kindergartners do not know their address or phone number yet. They can still learn that those facts are private.
- This lesson reuses sorting into categories from Lesson 1.
