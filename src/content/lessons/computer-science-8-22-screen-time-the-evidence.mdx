**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **8.IC.2** The student will analyze and evaluate the ramifications of screen time in one’s life.
  - a. Analyze scenarios or case studies to assess the impact of screen time on one’s physical and mental health.
  - b. Justify the argument that excessive screen time and video games can have significant consequences for the physical, emotional, and cognitive development of children and adolescents.

## Prep

- Write two or three brief case studies of fictional teens with different screen habits and outcomes, including one where screen use is heavy but mostly positive.
- Find and read a current summary on screen time and adolescent health from a reliable health organization, and print or bookmark it.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "Is all screen time the same? Is three hours of video calls with a grandparent the same as three hours of scrolling at midnight?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Teach case analysis: identify the amount, the type, and the timing of screen use; the effects on physical health such as sleep, activity, eyes, and posture; the effects on mental health such as mood, attention, and stress; and what else in the case could explain the outcome.
- Define the three areas of development. Physical: the body. Emotional: feelings and relationships. Cognitive: thinking, attention, and learning.
- Teach argument structure: a claim, evidence from sources and cases, reasoning that links them, and a response to the counterargument.
- Note what the evidence can and cannot show, and the difference between excessive use and use in general.

## Guided Practice (7 minutes)

1. The student analyzes one case study using the framework.
2. Compare it with the case where heavy use was mostly positive. What differs?
3. Read the health summary and pull out three pieces of evidence.
4. Outline the argument together: claim, evidence, reasoning, counterargument.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (5 minutes)

The student writes or presents the argument that excessive screen time and gaming can have significant consequences for physical, emotional, and cognitive development, using evidence from the source and the cases and answering one counterargument.

Look for:
- Cases are analyzed for type and timing as well as amount.
- All three areas of development are addressed.
- The argument uses evidence and answers a counterargument.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Analyze one case.
- Use an argument frame with sentence starters.

**For students ready for more**
- Evaluate the quality of the evidence in the health summary.
- Write the strongest opposing argument, then respond to it.

## Extensions

- Apply the framework to your own week.
- Present the argument to the family and discuss it.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- The standard asks the student to justify a specific argument. Teaching them to handle the counterargument keeps it honest.
- Use a current health source. Guidance in this area continues to develop.
