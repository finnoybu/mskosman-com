**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **K.DA.1** The student will gather and record data with or without a computing device.
  - a. Discuss the importance of data.
  - b. Identify numeric and non-numeric data.
  - c. Record data and communicate possible patterns.

## Prep

- Draw a recording sheet with one row for each color of item you will use.
- Choose the items so the colors have different counts and one color clearly has the most.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "Do we have more red blocks or more blue blocks in this pile? How could we find out for sure?" Let the student guess, then count one color together.

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Define: "Data is information we collect so we can learn something or answer a question."
- Say why it matters: "Data helps us know instead of guess. It helps us make choices."
- Explain two kinds:
  - "Some data is numbers, like how many blocks or how old you are. That is numeric data."
  - "Some data is not numbers, like colors, names, or favorite foods. That is non-numeric data."
- Give four quick examples and say which kind each is.
- Show the recording sheet. Model recording: pick up one item, find its row, and make one mark. "Recording means writing or marking data down so we do not forget it."
- No device is needed. Paper works just as well.

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. Together, record the pile of items: the student picks up one item at a time and makes one mark in the matching row.
2. Count the marks in each row and say the number.
3. Ask, "Is the color numeric or non-numeric? What about the count?"
4. Look at the sheet and ask, "What do you notice?" Help the student describe a pattern, such as "Red has the most" or "Two rows are the same."

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

Give a new, smaller question, such as "What colors are the crayons in this box?" or "How many windows, doors, and chairs are in this room?" The student records with marks on a fresh sheet and then tells you one thing they notice.

Also ask: "Why do people collect data?" and "Is three a number kind of data or a not-number kind?"

Look for:
- One mark for each item, in the correct row.
- The student tells numeric from non-numeric with examples.
- The student states something noticed about the data.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Use two rows and no more than ten items.
- Have the student place the actual item on the row before replacing it with a mark.
- Count aloud together.

**For students ready for more**
- Record with tally marks grouped in fives.
- Ask a "how many more" question about two rows.

## Extensions

- Record the weather each day for a week with a sun, cloud, or rain mark.
- If a device is available, take a photo of the finished recording sheet to save it.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- The standard says "with or without a computing device," so this lesson is fully unplugged by design.
- "Numeric" and "non-numeric" are the standard's words. Pair them with "numbers" and "not numbers" until the student is comfortable.
- "Communicate possible patterns" at this age means saying what they notice: most, least, same.
- Keep the recording sheet. It is used again in Lesson 12.
