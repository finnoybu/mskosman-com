**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **6.CSY.3** The student will identify and describe Artificial Intelligence (AI).
  - c. Define algorithmic bias and explain its consequences on AI technologies and systems.

## Prep

- Prepare a deliberately unbalanced training set for a made-up task, such as deciding who gets picked for a team, where nearly all examples of picked players are tall.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "If a coach only ever watched tall players, who would the coach think makes a good player?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Define algorithmic bias: systematic and unfair results from a computing system, often because of the data it learned from or choices made in its design.
- Name sources: training data that leaves groups out or reflects past unfairness, labels that carry people's assumptions, and goals that measure the wrong thing.
- Explain consequences: wrong or unfair decisions about people, loss of trust, harm that repeats at large scale, and results that are hard to notice because a computer seems neutral.
- Discuss responses: more representative data, testing results across groups, human review, and openness about how a system works.

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. Run the activity: the student learns the rule from the unbalanced set and applies it to new cards.
2. Ask, "Who was treated unfairly? Was that anyone's intention?"
3. Rebalance the data and repeat.
4. List two real areas where biased results would matter, such as hiring or lending.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student defines algorithmic bias, explains where it came from in the activity, and describes two consequences and one way to reduce it.

Look for:
- The definition includes unfairness that is systematic.
- The source of bias is traced to data or design.
- Consequences are about effects on people.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Use the coach comparison throughout.
- Discuss one consequence in depth.

**For students ready for more**
- Research a documented case of algorithmic bias with an adult and summarize it.
- Design a test to check a system for bias.

## Extensions

- Look for an example of a recommendation that seems skewed and suggest why.
- Explain algorithmic bias to a family member.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Bias here means a skew in results, usually unintended. It is a property of the system and its data.
- This lesson links to the training data lesson later in the year.
