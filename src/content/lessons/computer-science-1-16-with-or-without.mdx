**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **1.IC.1** The student will describe how computing technologies impact daily tasks and communication.
  - a. Determine when tasks should be completed with or without computing devices.
- **1.IC.3** The student will compare and contrast ways people complete tasks with and without computing technologies.
  - a. Identify tasks that can be completed with and without computing technologies.
  - b. Discuss advantages and disadvantages of using and not using computing technologies.

## Prep

- Write the eight task cards, each with a small drawing.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "How could you tell Grandma happy birthday?" Collect several ways. Point out that some use a device and some do not.

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Explain: "Many tasks can be done with computing technologies or without them. Neither way is always better."
- Define the words: "An advantage is something good about a choice. A disadvantage is something not so good."
- Model with one card, "draw a picture":
  - With a device: easy to erase and share. But it needs a charged device, and you cannot hang it up without printing.
  - Without: you can feel the crayons and hang it right away. But mistakes are harder to fix.
- Model deciding: "For a card for the refrigerator, I would choose paper. To send a picture to my aunt in another state, I would choose the tablet. It depends on the task."
- Give a guide: "Ask: What am I trying to do? Which way is safer, faster, or better for this?"

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. Take a card. Ask, "Can this be done with a device? Without one?" Record both ways in the columns.
2. For three cards, the student names one advantage and one disadvantage of each way.
3. Ask, "Which way would you choose here? Why?"
4. Find one task that is clearly better with a device (talking to a relative far away) and one that is clearly fine without (adding 2 plus 3).

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

Give two new tasks: "find a word's meaning" and "practice writing your name." For each, the student tells how to do it with and without a device, gives one advantage or disadvantage, and decides which way they would choose.

Look for:
- The student identifies both ways of doing a task.
- The student gives a real advantage or disadvantage.
- The decision comes with a reason tied to the task.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Use four cards.
- Act out both ways with real objects.
- Use "good thing" and "not so good thing" alongside the new words.

**For students ready for more**
- Find a task where the best choice changes depending on the situation.
- Interview a grown-up about how they did a task before they had a smartphone.

## Extensions

- For one day, notice each time someone in the family chooses a device or a non-device way of doing something.
- Do one task both ways, such as a shopping list on paper and on a phone, and compare.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- There are few wrong answers here. The goal is reasoning about the choice.
- This lesson joins two standards that ask almost the same question: when to use a device, and how tasks compare with and without one.
- Keep your own opinions light so the student does the comparing.
