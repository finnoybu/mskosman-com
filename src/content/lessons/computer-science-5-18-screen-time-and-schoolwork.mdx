**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **5.IC.2** The student will explain the potential impact of excessive screen time on academic performance.
  - a. Analyze data to determine the impact of screen time on academic performance.
  - b. Describe how academic behaviors that lead to academic success are impacted by daily screen time.
  - c. Differentiate usage of screen time that benefit and hinder academic performance.

## Prep

- Prepare a small made-up table for eight students: hours of recreational screen time on school nights, hours of sleep, and number of assignments finished. Label it clearly as sample data for practice.
- Write ten screen-use cards, such as researching a report, watching videos during homework, practicing math facts in an app, messaging friends late at night, reading an e-book, gaming before finishing work.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "What helps you do your best work? What gets in the way?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Introduce the sample data and say plainly that it is made up for practice.
- Model plotting screen time against assignments finished and describing the pattern.
- Name academic behaviors that lead to success: enough sleep, focused attention, finishing work, reading, and being prepared.
- Explain how daily screen time can affect them: later bedtimes, divided attention, and less time for work. Explain that some screen use supports learning.

## Guided Practice (7 minutes)

1. The student graphs screen time against sleep.
2. Ask, "What does the data suggest? What can it not prove?"
3. The student sorts the screen-use cards into helps and hinders.
4. Discuss cards that could go either way and what decides it.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student writes a conclusion from the data, describes how screen time can affect two academic behaviors, and gives two examples each of screen use that helps and hinders.

Look for:
- The conclusion matches the data and is stated cautiously.
- Academic behaviors are linked to screen habits.
- Examples are sorted with reasons.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Graph one pair of columns.
- Sort six cards.

**For students ready for more**
- Track your own screen time and one academic behavior for a week and analyze it.
- Find a published summary of research on the topic with an adult and compare.

## Extensions

- Create a study-time plan that separates helpful and distracting screen use.
- Try doing homework with the device in another room and compare.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- The practice data is invented. Say so, and avoid presenting it as research.
- A pattern in data suggests a link. It does not prove cause.
