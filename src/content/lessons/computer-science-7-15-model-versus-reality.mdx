**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **7.DA.1** The student will utilize computational tools to visualize and evaluate data to draw conclusions and make predictions.
  - c. Analyze patterns and trends within observed data, comparing them with the predictions made by computational models.
  - d. Evaluate the effectiveness and accuracy of computational models in capturing and explaining the observed data.

## Prep

- Check how to plot two data series on one chart in your spreadsheet program.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "If a model is right about most points but wrong at the end, is it a good model? What does it depend on?"

## Direct Instruction (4 minutes)

- Plot observed data and model predictions on the same chart.
- Analyze the observed data on its own first: its overall trend and any pattern such as leveling off.
- Compare: where does the model match, and where does it differ? Is the difference small and random, or does it grow in one direction?
- Define two criteria. Accuracy: how close predictions are to observations. Effectiveness: whether the model is useful for its purpose and explains why the data behaves as it does.

## Guided Practice (8 minutes)

1. The student creates the combined chart.
2. Describe the trend in the observed data.
3. Calculate the difference between predicted and observed values at each point.
4. Identify where the model is weakest and suggest a reason.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student writes an evaluation: how accurate the model is, with numbers; where it fails; whether it is effective for its purpose; and one improvement.

Look for:
- Trends are described from the data.
- The comparison uses calculated differences.
- The evaluation addresses both accuracy and usefulness.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Compare by eye using the chart.
- Use the frame: "The model is close when ___ and off when ___."

**For students ready for more**
- Calculate an average error and use it to compare two versions of the model.
- Use the model to predict a new case and test the prediction.

## Extensions

- Evaluate a prediction from a news source against what happened.
- Discuss why a simple model can be more useful than a complicated one.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- All models are simplifications. The question is whether one is accurate enough for its purpose.
- A difference that grows steadily usually means a missing variable or a wrong relationship.
