**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **5.CYB.1** The student will identify ways to limit unauthorized access on computing devices.
  - c. Describe the role of human interactions in social engineering attacks.
  - d. Identify ways to protect personal and private information when using a computing device and the Internet.
  - e. Explain the importance of updating software.

## Prep

- Write three role-play cards: a caller claiming to be tech support who needs a password; a message from a "friend" asking for a code that was just texted; a stranger in a game offering free items for account details.
- Find where your device shows its software version and update status.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "Is it easier to break a strong lock or to talk someone into opening the door?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Define social engineering: "Tricking people into giving up information or access, instead of breaking into the technology."
- Explain the human side: attackers use trust, fear, urgency, curiosity, and the wish to be helpful. They may pretend to be a friend, a company, or someone in authority.
- List protections: slow down, verify through another channel, never share passwords or codes, use strong unique passwords and two-step sign-in, adjust privacy settings, and ask a trusted adult.
- Explain updates: software has weaknesses that are discovered over time. Updates fix them. An out-of-date device has known holes that attackers can use.

## Guided Practice (7 minutes)

1. Role-play each card. The student names the feeling being used and responds safely.
2. Ask, "How could you check whether the request is real?"
3. Look at the device's update screen and discuss what it shows.
4. Start a personal protection plan with five practices.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student finishes the protection plan, explains how one social engineering attack works on people, and explains to you why updates should not be put off.

Look for:
- The student identifies the human reaction an attack depends on.
- Protections are practical and accurate.
- Updates are explained as fixing known weaknesses.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Role-play one card several times.
- Use the rule: stop, check, ask.

**For students ready for more**
- Write a short story where a character spots and stops a social engineering attempt.
- Find out how your family's devices are set to update.

## Extensions

- Share the protection plan with the family.
- Agree on a family code word for checking that a message really comes from a family member.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Attackers rely on urgency. Practicing a pause is the best defense.
- Installing updates remains an adult's task. The student should understand why it matters.
