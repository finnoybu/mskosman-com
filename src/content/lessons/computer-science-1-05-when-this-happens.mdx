**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **1.AP.2** The student will plan and implement algorithms that include the use of sequence and an event based on a predetermined task.
  - c. Identify and explain the role of events that are used in an algorithm.
  - d. Test algorithms that are sequential and contain an event.

## Prep

- Write WHEN on a card with a picture of a bell.
- Prepare three step cards for a simple action sequence, such as stand up, spin around, sit down.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Play freeze dance for one minute: the student moves while you hum and freezes when you ring the bell. Ask, "What told you to freeze?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Explain: "An event is something that happens that tells an algorithm to start or to do something."
- Give everyday examples: when the doorbell rings, someone opens the door. When the timer beeps, the cookies come out. When you press a button, the light turns on.
- Show the WHEN card. "The event is the signal. Nothing happens until the event happens."
- Lay out the algorithm: WHEN the bell rings, stand up, spin around, sit down.
- Point out the two parts: the event (the bell) and the sequence (three steps in order).
- Explain testing: "To test it, we make the event happen and watch whether every step happens in order."

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. Test the algorithm. Ring the bell. The student carries out the steps.
2. Ask, "What was the event? What did the event do?"
3. Test again without ringing the bell. Wait. Ask, "Why is nothing happening?"
4. Change the event together (WHEN I clap twice) and test again.
5. Swap two step cards and test. Ask, "Did it still do what we wanted?"

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (5 minutes)

The student builds a new algorithm with their own event and three steps, tells you what the event is and what it does, and then tests it with you performing the steps.

Look for:
- The student identifies the event in an algorithm.
- The student explains that the event starts or triggers the steps.
- The student tests by causing the event and checking each step in order.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Use one event and one action first ("When I clap, you jump").
- Keep the same event for the whole lesson.
- Use picture cards for every step.

**For students ready for more**
- Use two different events that start two different sequences.
- Combine with Lesson 4: add a REPEAT step inside the sequence.

## Extensions

- Go on an event hunt at home: find five things that wait for an event, such as a doorbell, a microwave, or an automatic door.
- If the student uses a beginner coding app, find the block that starts a program and name its event.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- The idea to secure is the role of the event: it is the trigger, separate from the steps that follow.
- Testing without the event (step 3 of Guided Practice) is the clearest way to show that role.
- No device is needed. The same idea appears later as "when clicked" or "when the key is pressed" in coding tools.
