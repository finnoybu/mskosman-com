**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **5.IC.4** The student will observe and examine intellectual property rights when considering the use of open-source licenses and copyrights.
  - a. Distinguish between open-source licenses and copyrights.
  - b. Research risks associated with inappropriate use of various digital information sources.
  - c. Describe and use strategies to protect online digital content and resources.

## Prep

- Find one example with a clear open license and one marked all rights reserved, and bookmark the license statements.
- Pick a document, drawing, or program the student created.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "Some software is free for anyone to use and change. Why would its creators allow that?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Review copyright: the creator controls copying and use, and others need permission.
- Define open source: "The creator shares the source code or work under a license that lets others use, study, change, and share it," usually with conditions such as giving credit or sharing changes the same way.
- Clarify that open source does not mean no rules. The license states them. Read the two license statements together.
- Explain risks of using sources inappropriately: breaking the law or a license, plagiarism and its academic consequences, unreliable information, and malware from unofficial downloads.
- List strategies to protect digital content: add your name and a copyright or license notice, save backups, control sharing settings, and use strong passwords on accounts that hold your work.

## Guided Practice (7 minutes)

1. The student compares the two examples: what may you do with each?
2. Together, look up one risk of downloading from unofficial sources and summarize it.
3. The student adds a name, date, and notice to their own file.
4. Check its sharing settings and make a backup.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student explains the difference between open source and copyright, names two risks of misusing digital sources, and shows the protections applied to their own work.

Look for:
- Open source is described as licensed sharing with conditions.
- Risks are specific.
- Protection strategies are actually applied.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Compare with the anchors "ask first" and "allowed, with rules."
- Apply one protection strategy.

**For students ready for more**
- Choose a license for your own project and explain the choice.
- Find an open-source program your family uses.

## Extensions

- Add license or credit notes to past projects.
- Set up a regular backup routine with an adult.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- The standard asks the student to use protection strategies, not only describe them.
- License details vary. Read the actual statement and avoid assuming.
