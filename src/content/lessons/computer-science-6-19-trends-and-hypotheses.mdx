**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **6.DA.3** The student will make predictions and draw conclusions from data visualizations.
  - b. Use computational tools to analyze patterns within data sets and identify trends.
  - c. Draw conclusions and make predictions based on the analysis and interpretation of the data visualization.
  - d. Utilize simulations and models to formulate, refine, and test hypotheses.

## Prep

- Build a small model in a spreadsheet, such as savings growing with a weekly deposit, where changing one input cell changes the results and a chart.
- Check how to sort, filter, and add a trend line or average.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "If you saved 5 dollars a week, how much would you have in a year? What if you saved 7? How could you find out without waiting a year?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Show tools for finding patterns: sorting, filtering, calculating averages, and adding a trend line to a chart.
- Define trend: the general direction of data over time or across values.
- Review conclusions and predictions: a conclusion states what the data shows, and a prediction extends the trend with a stated degree of confidence.
- Define hypothesis: a testable statement about what will happen. Show the model: change an input, observe the result, and compare it with the hypothesis. Refine and test again.

## Guided Practice (8 minutes)

1. The student uses sorting or an average to describe a pattern in their data.
2. The student adds a trend line and states the trend.
3. The student writes a hypothesis about the prepared model, then tests it by changing the input.
4. The student refines the hypothesis and tests once more.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student writes one conclusion and one prediction from their own visualization, and reports the hypothesis tested with the model, the result, and how it was refined.

Look for:
- A computational tool is used to find the pattern.
- Conclusions and predictions are tied to the visualization.
- The hypothesis is tested and revised based on results.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Use the prepared model only.
- Provide the frame: "If ___ increases, then ___ will ___."

**For students ready for more**
- Add a second input to the model and test how the two interact.
- Explain the limits of the model: what does it ignore?

## Extensions

- Model a real goal, such as saving for a purchase.
- Find an online simulation in science and test a hypothesis with it.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- A model lets you test ideas that would be slow, costly, or impossible to test in real life.
- Ask what the model leaves out. Every model simplifies.
