**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **8.AP.1** The student will apply computational thinking to construct programs to accomplish a task as a means of creative expression or scientific exploration.
  - a. Identify patterns and repeated steps in an algorithm, problem, or process.
  - b. Decompose an algorithm, problem, or process into sub-components.
  - c. Abstract relevant information to identify essential details.

## Prep

- Ask the student to bring an idea for a substantial program: an interactive story or game, a data explorer, or a simulation of a system with several interacting parts.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "What is the difference between an idea for a program and a program you could actually finish in three weeks?"

## Direct Instruction (4 minutes)

- Introduce scope: deciding what the program will and will not do.
- Decompose the idea into sub-components and then into the functions each needs.
- Find patterns: repeated steps that become loops or shared functions, and similar objects that share a structure.
- Abstract: decide what data represents each thing in the program and what is left out. State the assumptions.
- Separate the essential version, the smallest program that fulfills the purpose, from features to add later.

## Guided Practice (8 minutes)

1. The student writes the purpose in one sentence.
2. Build a decomposition tree to two levels.
3. Mark repeated patterns and name the shared functions.
4. List the data needed for each main element and draw a line around the essential version.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student produces a scoping document: purpose, decomposition tree, repeated patterns, data abstractions with stated assumptions, and the boundary of the essential version.

Look for:
- Decomposition is complete to two levels.
- Patterns are tied to loops or functions.
- The essential version is realistic.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Scope a small program with three sub-components.
- Decompose together.

**For students ready for more**
- Identify dependencies and a build order.
- Write two alternative abstractions and choose between them.

## Extensions

- Review how a published game or simulation scopes its first version.
- Have someone challenge the scope and defend or adjust it.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- A finished small program teaches more than an unfinished ambitious one.
- Keep this document. It drives Lessons 2 through 6.
