**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **8.IC.1** The student will assess the social impacts and ethical considerations of computing technologies.
  - a. Analyze the impact of sharing data through computing technologies.
  - b. Critique the role the Internet plays in social life, the global economy, and culture.

## Prep

- Choose one everyday action to trace, such as ordering food through an app, using a fitness tracker, or signing up for a free game.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "When you use a free app, what do you pay with?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Trace the data in the chosen action: what is collected, who receives it, who it is passed to, and how long it is kept.
- Analyze impact for individuals, such as convenience and personalization against loss of privacy and profiling; for organizations, such as better services against the responsibility to protect data; and for society, such as research and public health against surveillance and concentration of power.
- Define critique: a reasoned judgment that weighs strengths and weaknesses with evidence.
- Frame the three areas for the Internet: social life, the global economy, and culture.

## Guided Practice (7 minutes)

1. The student draws the data-flow map for the chosen action.
2. Mark each point where data is shared and with whom.
3. List two benefits and two harms of that sharing.
4. Fill in the critique chart for the three areas: strengths, weaknesses, and evidence.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student writes a short critique of the Internet's role in one of the three areas, with at least two strengths, two weaknesses, and a conclusion, and summarizes the impact of the data sharing they mapped.

Look for:
- Data flows are traced beyond the first recipient.
- Impacts are analyzed for more than one group.
- The critique weighs both sides and reaches a judgment.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Map a simple action with two data recipients.
- Critique one area with one strength and one weakness.

**For students ready for more**
- Read the data-sharing section of a real privacy policy and compare it with the map.
- Critique all three areas and find where they conflict.

## Extensions

- Review the permissions of the apps on a family device.
- Discuss the critique with someone who disagrees.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- A critique is not a complaint. It needs strengths, weaknesses, and evidence.
- Tracing where data goes after the first company often surprises students.
