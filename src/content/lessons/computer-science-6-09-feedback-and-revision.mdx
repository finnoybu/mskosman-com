**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **6.AP.3** The student will use the iterative design process to create, test, and debug programs using a block-based or text-based programming language.
  - f. Incorporate feedback from others to refine program.
  - g. Revise and improve programs to resolve errors and produce desired outcomes.

## Prep

- Arrange for a family member or friend to try the program.
- Make a feedback form: What worked well? What was confusing? What did not work? One suggestion.

## Warm-up (2 minutes)

Ask, "Have you ever used an app where something obvious was missing? How do you think the makers find out?"

## Direct Instruction (4 minutes)

- Explain why feedback matters: the programmer knows how the program is supposed to be used. Other people reveal what is unclear or broken.
- Give rules for receiving feedback: watch without explaining, write down what is said, and ask follow-up questions.
- Explain sorting feedback: errors to fix, improvements worth making, and suggestions to set aside, with a reason.
- Review revising: one change at a time, with testing after each.

## Guided Practice (9 minutes)

1. The tester uses the program while the student observes and takes notes.
2. The tester completes the feedback form.
3. The student sorts the feedback and chooses two changes.
4. The student makes the first change and tests.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (5 minutes)

The student makes the second change, retests the whole program, and writes a short change log stating what feedback was received, what was changed, and why.

Look for:
- Feedback is recorded accurately.
- Choices about which feedback to act on are justified.
- The program is retested after revision.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Collect feedback from one person on one feature.
- Make one change.

**For students ready for more**
- Collect feedback from a second tester after revising and compare.
- Give constructive feedback on someone else's project.

## Extensions

- Write helpful feedback on a game or app you use.
- Keep a change log for future projects.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Watching silently while someone struggles with your program is hard and valuable.
- Not all feedback must be followed, but each piece deserves a reasoned decision.
