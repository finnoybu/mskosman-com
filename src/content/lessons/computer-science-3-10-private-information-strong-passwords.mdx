**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **3.CYB.1** The student will apply safe practices to protect private information.
  - a. Identify and distinguish personal information that should be private.
  - b. Describe the importance of using a strong password.
  - c. Create and use strong passwords to protect private information.

## Prep

- Write twelve cards: full name, home address, phone number, birthday, school or co-op name, password, a photo of your face, favorite sport, favorite book, first name only, pet's name, the town's weather.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "What could a stranger do if they knew your address and when your family was away?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Define personal information: facts about you. Some of it is private because it could be used to find you, pretend to be you, or get into your accounts.
- Sort three cards aloud. Note that some items, such as a pet's name, seem harmless but are often used as passwords or security answers.
- Explain why strength matters: a strong password is the main barrier between other people and your private information.
- Give a recipe: at least 12 characters, several unrelated words, a number and a symbol, and nothing about you. Model one, such as Lantern!Otter42Sky.

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. The student sorts the remaining cards and explains the harder ones.
2. Together, list what makes a password weak.
3. The student creates two practice passphrases using the recipe.
4. Test them: could someone who knows you guess it? Is it long enough?

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (5 minutes)

The student creates one more strong practice password and explains why it is strong. Then the student names three kinds of personal information that should be private and why.

Look for:
- Private items are identified with reasons.
- The student explains how a strong password protects information.
- The created password is long, mixed, and not based on personal facts.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Sort eight cards.
- Build a passphrase by drawing three random word cards from a pile.

**For students ready for more**
- Explain why a different password is needed for each account.
- Find out what a password manager does.

## Extensions

- With a parent, update one real family password to a stronger one. The student suggests the recipe but does not need to know the result.
- Make a "keep it private" reminder card.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Use practice passwords only in the lesson. Real ones are created privately with you.
- The standard says "create and use." The extension with a parent covers real use safely.
