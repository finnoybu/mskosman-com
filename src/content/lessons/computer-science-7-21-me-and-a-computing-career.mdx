**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **7.IC.3** The student will identify individual preferences, skillset, and experiences and determine how these relate to a chosen computer science career field.
  - a. Use a career interest assessment to identify and categorize preferences, skillsets, and experiences.
  - b. Evaluate and connect personal skillsets, interests, talents, and values to a computer science career.

## Prep

- Find a free, reputable career interest assessment for middle school students. State career and education agencies often provide one. Try it first.
- Gather short descriptions of computing fields: software development, cybersecurity, data science, user experience design, networking, game development, AI.

## Warm-up (2 minutes)

Ask, "What kinds of activities make you lose track of time? What would you rather avoid?"

## Direct Instruction (3 minutes)

- Explain a career interest assessment: a set of questions that groups your preferences into categories and suggests matching fields. It is a starting point, not a verdict.
- Define the four things to identify. Interests: what you enjoy. Skills: what you can do. Experiences: what you have done. Values: what matters to you in work.
- Explain connecting: compare your categories with what each computing field involves day to day.

## Guided Practice (10 minutes)

1. The student completes the assessment.
2. Record the results on the note sheet under the four headings, adding the student's own observations.
3. Read the computing field descriptions.
4. Match the student's profile to one or two fields and note where it fits and where it does not.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student writes a short reflection: their main interests, skills, experiences, and values; the computing career that fits best; and the evidence for the match.

Look for:
- The assessment is completed and results categorized.
- Personal traits are described honestly.
- The connection to a career is supported by specifics.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Complete a shorter assessment or do it together.
- Compare with two fields.

**For students ready for more**
- Identify a skill gap for the chosen career and plan how to close it.
- Interview someone in that field.

## Extensions

- Retake the assessment in a year and compare.
- Try a beginner activity in the chosen field.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Results will change as the student grows. Treat them as a snapshot.
- A poor match is useful information too.
