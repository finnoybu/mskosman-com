**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **4.DA.2** The student will create and evaluate data representations to make predictions and conclusions.
  - c. Recognize and analyze patterns and relationships within data sets.
  - d. Analyze visual representations to make predictions and draw conclusions.

## Prep

- Prepare a small two-column table where one value tends to rise with the other, and a line graph of it.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Show the prepared table. Ask, "When one of these goes up, what happens to the other?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Define a pattern in data: something that repeats or changes in a regular way, such as rising, falling, or cycling.
- Define a relationship: when two kinds of data change together. More daylight tends to go with higher temperature.
- Caution: two things changing together does not prove that one causes the other.
- Model reading the line graph: describe its shape, draw a conclusion, and predict the next value.

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. The student describes the pattern in the prepared graph.
2. Ask, "What relationship do you see? Could something else explain it?"
3. Turn to the student's own chart. Identify a pattern.
4. Make one prediction and state what data would test it.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student writes two sentences about their own data: one conclusion and one prediction, each pointing to evidence in the chart.

Look for:
- Patterns are described with words such as increases, decreases, or repeats.
- A relationship between two kinds of data is recognized.
- Conclusions and predictions cite the visual.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Use a graph with a clear upward trend.
- Use the frames "As ___ goes up, ___ goes ___" and "I predict ___ because ___."

**For students ready for more**
- Find a data set where two things have no relationship.
- Explain why ice cream sales and sunburns rise together without one causing the other.

## Extensions

- Test the prediction by collecting more data.
- Find a graph in the news and describe the relationship it shows.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- The caution about cause is worth a minute even at this age.
- Point to the chart whenever the student makes a claim.
