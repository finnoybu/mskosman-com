**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **K.CSY.1** The student will identify the purpose and components of a computing device.
  - a. Identify and locate parts of a computing device, including sensors.
  - b. Describe the function of common components in a computing device.

## Prep

- Charge the device and leave it powered off.
- Find its camera, microphone, and speaker ahead of time. They can be hard to spot.

## Warm-up (2 minutes)

Place the device in front of the student, turned off. Ask, "What is this? What parts can you see?" Let the student point and use their own words.

Say, "A machine like this is called a computing device. Today we will learn the names of its parts and the job each part does."

## Direct Instruction (6 minutes)

Point to each part on the real device, name it, and give its job in one short sentence. Cover the parts your device has:

- **Screen**: shows pictures and words.
- **Keyboard**: lets us type letters and numbers.
- **Mouse or touchpad**: lets us point and choose things.
- **Speaker**: lets sound come out.
- **Power button**: turns the device on and off.

Then introduce sensors: "Some parts notice things around the device. These parts are called sensors."

- **Camera**: notices light so it can take a picture.
- **Microphone**: notices sound, like your voice.
- **Touchscreen**: notices where your finger touches.

Turn the device on and show one sensor working, such as the screen responding to a touch.

## Guided Practice (5 minutes)

1. Say a part name. The student finds it and places a sticky note on or beside it.
2. Reverse it: point to a part and the student names it.
3. For each part, ask, "What is its job?"
4. Ask, "Which parts are sensors? What does each one notice?"

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

Remove the sticky notes. Ask the student to give you a "tour" of the device: point to each part, say its name, and say what it does. Then ask them to point to one sensor and tell what it notices.

Look for:
- The student locates and names at least four parts.
- The student describes the job of at least three parts.
- The student identifies at least one sensor.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Teach three parts and one sensor, and add more in a later session.
- Pair each part with a gesture (typing fingers for keyboard, cupped ear for microphone).
- Accept pointing plus the job in the student's own words.

**For students ready for more**
- Compare two different devices and find the same parts on each.
- Sort the parts into two groups: parts that take something in and parts that put something out.

## Extensions

- The student draws a device and tells you the parts to label.
- Look for the camera, microphone, and speaker on a phone or another device at home.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Teach the parts that are actually on your device. A tablet has no separate keyboard or mouse, and that is fine.
- The standard specifically includes sensors, so do not skip them. Camera, microphone, and touchscreen are the easiest for this age to see working.
- Supervise handling throughout. Proper care of devices is taught directly in Lesson 8.
