**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **6.AP.1** The student will apply computational thinking to identify patterns, make use of decomposition to break down problems or processes into sub-components, and design algorithms.
  - c. Abstract relevant information to identify essential details.
  - d. Design algorithms using abstraction to accomplish a task or express a computational process.

## Prep

- Write a scenario full of detail, such as a school store that sells five items, with staff names, opening times, colors of packaging, prices, and stock counts, and a task: decide what to reorder each week.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "When you give someone directions, what do you leave out? Why does leaving things out make the directions better?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Define abstraction: representing a problem by its essential details and hiding the rest.
- Model with the scenario: for reordering, only item, stock count, and a minimum level matter.
- Show abstraction in an algorithm: write "FOR EACH item: IF stock is below minimum THEN add to order." It works for any item because the specifics are replaced with general names.
- Show a second layer: call a named step such as "calculate order amount" without spelling out its details at the top level.

## Guided Practice (7 minutes)

1. The student lists the essential details for a different task in the same scenario, such as scheduling staff.
2. Together, write an algorithm using general names, not specific values.
3. Test it with two different sets of data.
4. Ask, "What did we hide, and why is it safe to hide it?"

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student writes an algorithm for a new task using abstraction: general names for data and at least one named sub-step. Then the student explains what was left out.

Look for:
- Essential details are separated from irrelevant ones.
- The algorithm works for different inputs.
- A named sub-step hides lower-level detail.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Highlight essential details together first.
- Convert a specific worked example into a general one.

**For students ready for more**
- Add a second level of named sub-steps.
- Explain how a map app uses abstraction.

## Extensions

- Write a general algorithm for a household routine that works for any family member.
- Find three abstractions in daily life: icons, maps, a remote control.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- An algorithm that names specific values is a solved example. One that uses general names is an abstraction.
- The four parts of computational thinking are now in place.
