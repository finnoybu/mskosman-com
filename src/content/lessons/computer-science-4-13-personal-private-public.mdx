**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **4.CYB.2** The student will identify and investigate best practices to safeguard information shared online and through online platforms.
  - a. Classify personal, private, and public information.
  - b. Research and evaluate tradeoffs of sharing information.

## Prep

- Write fifteen cards, such as favorite food, hobbies, first name, full name, home address, password, birthday, phone number, a photo in a team uniform, your town's name, the library's opening hours, a news headline, medical information, a pet's name, your school or co-op.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "What is something about you that you would tell anyone? Something you would tell only family? Why the difference?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Define the three groups. Personal: about you but not enough to identify or find you, such as hobbies. Private: could identify, locate, or harm you, such as an address or password. Public: available to anyone and not about you in particular, such as store hours.
- Note that pieces add up: several personal facts together can identify a person.
- Define tradeoff: giving up one thing to gain another.
- Model weighing one: sharing a photo of a game win gives fun and connection, but the uniform shows the team name and where you play.

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. The student sorts the cards and explains the harder ones.
2. Find two personal cards that become identifying when combined.
3. Choose two things people often share. List a benefit and a risk for each.
4. Ask, "Is it worth it? What could make it safer?"

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (5 minutes)

The student classifies five items you name and evaluates the tradeoff of one sharing decision, stating what they would do and why.

Look for:
- Items are classified with reasons.
- The student recognizes that combined facts can identify someone.
- The decision weighs both a benefit and a risk.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Sort into two groups first: private and not private.
- Evaluate one tradeoff together.

**For students ready for more**
- Look at a sign-up form with an adult and decide which fields are necessary.
- Research why apps ask for location and weigh the tradeoff.

## Extensions

- Review what a family photo shows before it is shared.
- Make a "think before you share" checklist.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- The standard says "research and evaluate." Reading a sign-up form or a privacy summary with you counts as research.
- Many items are not clear-cut. The reasoning matters more than the pile.
