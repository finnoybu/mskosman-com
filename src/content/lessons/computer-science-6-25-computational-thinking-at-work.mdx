**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **6.IC.3** The student will explore career pathways and identify how computer science and computational thinking practices align with these pathways.
  - a. Investigate a career of interest and determine how computer science and computational thinking practices are used in the chosen career.

## Prep

- Ask the student in advance which career interests them. It does not need to be a technology career.
- Bookmark a reliable career information page for it.

## Warm-up (2 minutes)

Ask, "Does a chef use computational thinking? A nurse? A carpenter?"

## Direct Instruction (4 minutes)

- Review the four practices: decomposition, pattern recognition, abstraction, and algorithm design.
- Show they appear everywhere: a chef decomposes a menu, a nurse follows and adapts protocols, a carpenter abstracts a plan from a sketch.
- Distinguish computer science used directly, such as software, data, and automation, from computational thinking used as a way of solving problems.
- Model note-taking with one career: tasks, tools, computer science used, and computational thinking used.

## Guided Practice (9 minutes)

1. The student researches the chosen career's main tasks.
2. Identify the computing tools used.
3. For three tasks, name the computational thinking practice involved.
4. If possible, ask a person in the field one or two questions.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student writes or presents a short profile: the career, how computer science is used in it, and three examples of computational thinking in its daily work.

Look for:
- The career is accurately described.
- Computer science uses are specific.
- Each computational thinking example is matched to the right practice.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Provide the four practices as a checklist.
- Research one task in depth.

**For students ready for more**
- Compare two careers and their use of computational thinking.
- Find out what education path leads to the career.

## Extensions

- Interview someone in the career.
- Shadow a family member at work and note computational thinking in action.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Let the student's real interest drive the choice.
- Pathways means the steps toward a career. A brief look at education and training fits here.
