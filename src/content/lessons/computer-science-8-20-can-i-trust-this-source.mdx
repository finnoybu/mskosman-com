**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **8.IC.1** The student will assess the social impacts and ethical considerations of computing technologies.
  - c. Evaluate online and print sources for credibility and reliability.

## Prep

- Gather the sources, as printouts or bookmarks, on a topic the student is studying.
- Prepare a checklist: Who wrote it and what are their qualifications? Who published it and why? When? What evidence and sources does it give? Do other reliable sources agree? Is the tone balanced?

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Show two sources that disagree on a fact. Ask, "How would you decide which to believe?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Define credibility: whether the author and publisher can be trusted on the topic. Define reliability: whether the information is accurate and consistent with other good sources.
- Walk through the checklist with one source.
- Teach lateral reading: leave the source and check what others say about it and about its claims.
- Note that print is not automatically reliable and online is not automatically unreliable. The same questions apply. Point out warning signs: no author, no date, no sources, emotional language, and a purpose of selling.

## Guided Practice (7 minutes)

1. The student evaluates two online sources with the checklist.
2. Evaluate the print source the same way.
3. Rank all the sources from most to least trustworthy and justify the order.
4. Check one claim by finding it in two other sources.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student evaluates a new source of their own choosing and writes a short verdict: credible or not, reliable or not, and the evidence for each.

Look for:
- Credibility and reliability are judged separately.
- The checklist is applied with evidence.
- Claims are verified against other sources.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Evaluate two sources with a shortened checklist.
- Evaluate together, then independently.

**For students ready for more**
- Evaluate a source that looks professional but is unreliable and identify how it misleads.
- Trace a widely shared claim back to its origin.

## Extensions

- Evaluate the sources used in a recent school project.
- Teach the checklist to a family member.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Checking what others say about a source is faster and more accurate than studying the source alone.
- Choose a topic without strong emotions attached so the method stays in focus.
