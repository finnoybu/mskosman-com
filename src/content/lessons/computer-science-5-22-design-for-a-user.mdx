**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **5.IC.5** The student will examine the effects of social interactions due to computing technologies.
  - d. Create an artifact that illustrates a solution to address the need or want of a user.

## Prep

- Help the student choose a real user: a grandparent, a younger sibling, a neighbor, or a friend.
- If possible, arrange a short conversation with that person before or during the lesson.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "What is something that annoys or slows down someone you know when they use technology?"

## Direct Instruction (4 minutes)

- Define artifact: something you create, such as a sketch, a paper prototype, a storyboard, a poster, or a program.
- Teach the steps: understand the user, define the need or want in one sentence, brainstorm ideas, choose one, and illustrate it.
- Model a need statement: "Grandpa needs a way to see family photos without tapping through small menus."
- Show a paper prototype: screens drawn on paper with large buttons and labels.

## Guided Practice (9 minutes)

1. The student interviews the user or recalls what they know and writes a need statement.
2. Brainstorm at least five ideas on sticky notes.
3. Choose one and list the features that respond to the user's needs.
4. Begin the artifact.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student finishes the artifact and presents it, explaining who the user is, what they need, and how the design addresses it.

Look for:
- The need statement names a specific user and need.
- The artifact clearly illustrates the solution.
- Design choices are tied to the user's needs.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Design for yourself or a sibling.
- Provide a screen template to draw on.

**For students ready for more**
- Show the artifact to the user, gather feedback, and revise.
- Build a working version in the programming tool.

## Extensions

- Design a second solution for a different user and compare.
- Keep the artifact in a portfolio.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- The artifact can be entirely on paper.
- Feedback from the real user teaches more than any other step.
