**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **5.IC.5** The student will examine the effects of social interactions due to computing technologies.
  - a. List and explain how advances in computing technologies impact communication and collaboration.
  - b. Describe how computing technologies can be designed to engage and interact with users including those with diverse needs.
  - c. Evaluate activities conducted in the physical and online environments.

## Prep

- Find accessibility settings on a device: text size, screen reader, captions, voice control, high contrast.
- Write five activity cards, such as a club meeting, a group project, a music lesson, a game with friends, a birthday celebration.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "How would you work on a project with someone who lives 500 miles away? How would that have worked 40 years ago?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- List advances and their effects: messaging and video calls make communication instant across distance, shared documents let people edit together, and translation tools cross languages. Note downsides: misunderstanding without tone of voice, distraction, and less in-person time.
- Define diverse needs: people differ in vision, hearing, movement, language, age, and experience.
- Show design that responds: captions, screen readers, voice control, adjustable text, simple layouts, and multiple languages. Good design engages users and can be used by as many people as possible.
- Model evaluating an activity in both environments with a chart: what is gained and lost in person and online.

## Guided Practice (7 minutes)

1. The student explores two accessibility features and says who each helps.
2. The student lists three advances and one effect of each on collaboration.
3. Evaluate two activity cards in the chart.
4. Ask, "Which version would you choose, and when?"

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student evaluates one more activity in both environments, names two design features for diverse needs, and explains one way technology has changed collaboration.

Look for:
- Effects on communication and collaboration are specific.
- Design features are matched to user needs.
- The evaluation weighs both environments.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Explore one accessibility feature in depth.
- Evaluate one activity together.

**For students ready for more**
- Use a device with a screen reader or voice control for five minutes and describe the experience.
- Find an app with poor accessibility and suggest improvements.

## Extensions

- Turn on captions for a week and notice when they help.
- Collaborate on a shared document with a relative.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Accessibility features help many people, not only those with disabilities.
- Lesson 22 uses these ideas to design a solution.
