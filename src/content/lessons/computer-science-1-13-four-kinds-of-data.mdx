**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **1.DA.1** The student will explore how data can be stored and retrieved from various computing devices.
  - a. Identify data formats used for various purposes, including audio, images, text, and video.
  - b. Explore and identify computing devices that collect, store, and/or display data.

## Prep

- Make the four header cards and the eight example cards.
- On the device, find where photos and recordings are saved so you can open that place quickly.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "What is data?" Remind the student that data is information we collect. Then ask, "Have you ever looked at an old photo on a phone? Where was that photo kept all that time?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Explain: "Data comes in different forms. A form of data is called a format."
- Introduce the four formats with the header cards:
  - **Audio**: data you hear, like a song or a voice message.
  - **Image**: data you look at that does not move, like a photo.
  - **Text**: data made of letters and numbers, like a message or a list.
  - **Video**: moving pictures, usually with sound.
- Say what each is good for: "A photo shows what something looks like. A voice message lets you hear how someone sounds."
- Explain what devices do with data, using three words:
  - **Collect**: take data in. A camera collects images. A microphone collects audio.
  - **Store**: keep data for later.
  - **Display**: show or play data. A screen displays. A speaker plays.
- Demonstrate: take a photo (collect), close the camera, then open the saved photos and find it (store and retrieve). Show it on the screen (display).

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. The student sorts the eight example cards under the four format headers.
2. For each format, ask, "When would this format be the best choice?"
3. The student records a short voice clip on the device, then finds it and plays it back.
4. Ask for each step, "Was that collecting, storing, or displaying?"

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

Name four new examples (a recipe, a ringtone, a birthday party recording, a selfie). The student names the format of each. Then ask, "Name one device that collects data, one that stores data, and one that displays data." A single device may be the answer to more than one.

Look for:
- Each example is matched to the correct format.
- The student identifies devices that collect, store, and display.
- The student can find saved data again with little help.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Teach two formats at a time.
- Use real examples on the device in place of cards.
- Use gestures: cup an ear for audio, frame the eyes for image.

**For students ready for more**
- Discuss examples that mix formats, such as a picture book read aloud on video.
- Find other devices that collect data, such as a smartwatch counting steps or a digital thermometer.

## Extensions

- Make one piece of data in each format about the same topic, such as a pet: a photo, a recording of its sound, its name typed, and a short video.
- Look through family photos and talk about how long they have been stored.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- The standard is about exploring that data can be stored and retrieved. The moment the student finds their own photo or recording again is the heart of the lesson.
- You stay in control of the device and choose what is opened.
- Kindergarten sorted data as numbers and not numbers. Formats are a new way to group data.
