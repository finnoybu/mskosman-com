**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **3.IC.4** The student will demonstrate how to use information created by others with permission.
  - a. Discuss copyright, piracy, and plagiarism.
  - b. Demonstrate how to use information created by others.

## Prep

- Choose a short factual paragraph the student can read.
- Find the copyright page of a book.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Hold up the student's drawing. Say, "I am going to put my name on this and tell everyone I made it." Ask, "How does that feel? Why?"

## Direct Instruction (6 minutes)

- Define copyright: "The law that says the person who creates something decides how it can be copied and used." Show the copyright page and symbol.
- Define piracy: copying or sharing music, movies, games, or software without paying or without permission.
- Define plagiarism: presenting someone else's words or ideas as your own.
- Teach how to use others' work properly: ask permission or check that it is allowed, put ideas in your own words, use quotation marks for exact words, and give credit by naming the author and source.
- Model with the paragraph: restate one idea in your own words and write where it came from.

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. Sort four situations as copyright issue, piracy, plagiarism, or okay: copying a friend's report; quoting a sentence and naming the book; downloading a movie from an unofficial site; using your own photo.
2. The student restates a second idea from the paragraph in their own words.
3. The student writes a credit line: title, author, and where it was found.
4. Ask, "When would you need to ask permission?"

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student uses one fact from the paragraph in a sentence of their own and gives credit. Then the student explains the difference between piracy and plagiarism.

Look for:
- The three terms are explained correctly.
- The student's sentence is in their own words.
- A credit line is included.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Focus on plagiarism and giving credit first.
- Provide a credit line template.

**For students ready for more**
- Look for images that say they are free to reuse and find out what that means.
- Write a permission request for using someone's photo.

## Extensions

- Add credit lines to a past report or project.
- Find the copyright notice on a game, a movie, and a website.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- The standard says "with permission." Asking and crediting are both part of using others' work properly.
- Start from the student's own feelings as a creator. It makes the rules make sense.
