**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **5.NI.1** The student will identify and describe cloud computing.
  - a. Define cloud computing.
  - b. List examples of cloud computing.
  - c. List the advantages and disadvantages of cloud computing.
  - d. Identify safe practices and potential security risks when using cloud computing.

## Prep

- Make cards for services your family uses that rely on the cloud, such as online photo storage, email, shared documents, streaming video or music, and online game saves.
- Add two cards for things stored only on a device.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "If your tablet broke today, would your photos be gone? Where are they?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Define cloud computing: "Using computers and storage that belong to someone else, reached over the Internet, to store files and run programs." The cloud is real servers in large buildings called data centers.
- Connect to earlier lessons: your device is the client, and the cloud is made of servers.
- List examples using the cards.
- Chart advantages: reach files from any device, share and work together easily, automatic backup, and little storage needed on the device. Disadvantages: needs an Internet connection, may cost money, depends on the company, and your data is on someone else's computers.
- Give safe practices and risks. Practices: strong unique passwords, two-step sign-in, careful sharing settings, and signing out on shared devices. Risks: stolen passwords, sharing by mistake, and data breaches at the company.

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. The student sorts the cards into cloud and device only.
2. Complete the advantages and disadvantages chart together.
3. Ask, "What would you keep in the cloud? What would you not?"
4. The student lists three safe practices for a shared document.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student defines cloud computing, gives three examples, two advantages, two disadvantages, and two safe practices with the risk each one guards against.

Look for:
- The definition includes remote servers reached over the Internet.
- Examples are real cloud services.
- Each safe practice is linked to a risk.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Use the phrase "someone else's computer, reached through the Internet."
- Chart two advantages and two disadvantages.

**For students ready for more**
- Find out where a file in a family cloud account is shared and with whom.
- Explain what happens to cloud files when the Internet is down.

## Extensions

- List the cloud services your family uses and who has access.
- Check the sharing settings of one shared item with an adult.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Students often picture the cloud as nowhere. Stress that it is physical servers.
- This lesson draws together networks, security, and data storage.
