**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **7.CSY.1** The student will design projects that use computing devices to collect and exchange data.
  - d. Evaluate the usability of hardware and software to collect and exchange data.
  - e. Select the hardware and software components for project designs by considering factors such as functionality, cost, size, speed, accessibility, and aesthetics.

## Prep

- Gather basic facts on two or three options for the project's hardware and software: what each does, its cost, and any limits.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "Have you ever used something that worked but was annoying to use? What made it annoying?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Define usability: how easily and effectively the intended users can use something. Consider how easy it is to learn, how many steps tasks take, how errors are handled, and whether everyone who needs it can use it.
- Define the six factors. Functionality: does it do the job? Cost. Size. Speed. Accessibility: can people with different abilities use it? Aesthetics: how it looks and feels.
- Introduce a decision matrix: options in rows, factors in columns, a score for each, and weights for the factors that matter most.
- Model scoring one option.

## Guided Practice (7 minutes)

1. The student evaluates the usability of each option for the project's users.
2. Decide which factors matter most for this project and why.
3. Complete the decision matrix.
4. Select the components and note the tradeoffs accepted.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student writes a short justification: the components chosen, how they scored on the six factors, and what was given up.

Look for:
- Usability is judged from the user's point of view.
- All six factors are considered.
- The selection is justified with tradeoffs stated.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Compare two options on three factors.
- Score together and let the student decide.

**For students ready for more**
- Test an option with a real user and revise the scores.
- Redo the matrix with different weights and see whether the choice changes.

## Extensions

- Evaluate the usability of an app or device at home.
- Use a decision matrix for a real purchase decision.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- There is rarely a perfect option. Naming what was traded away is the skill.
- Accessibility is a factor in its own right, not an afterthought.
