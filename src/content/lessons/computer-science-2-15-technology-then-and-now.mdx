**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **2.IC.1** The student will examine the positive and negative impacts of how using computing technologies has changed the way people live, work, and interact.
  - a. Identify current uses of computing/emerging technologies and discuss how they impact society.
  - c. Model healthy habits for using computing technologies.

## Prep

- Gather three then-and-now pairs.
- If possible, ask an older relative to share one memory of life before smartphones.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Show a paper map and a map app, or pictures of them. Ask, "How did people find their way before phones? What is better now? Is anything worse?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Explain: "Computing technologies have changed how people live, work, and talk with each other. Some changes are positive and some are negative."
- Work through one pair, recording a positive and a negative effect in the two columns.
- Introduce emerging technologies: "Emerging means new and still growing." Give examples a child may have seen: voice assistants, self-checkout machines, delivery robots, cars that help with steering.
- Model healthy habits yourself: put the device away during the conversation, sit with good posture, and set the timer for a movement break.
- Say, "A healthy habit is something I do, not just something I know."

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. For the other two pairs, the student names how life changed and one positive and one negative effect.
2. Ask, "What is one new technology you have noticed? How does it affect people?"
3. When the timer rings, the student leads a stretch and a look-away break.
4. The student shows how to set up a comfortable, healthy place to use a device.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student names two current uses of technology, gives a positive and a negative effect for one, and demonstrates two healthy habits.

Look for:
- The student identifies current or emerging technologies.
- The student discusses both positive and negative effects.
- Healthy habits are shown, not only described.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Use one then-and-now pair and act out both versions.
- Practice one healthy habit daily for a week.

**For students ready for more**
- Interview a grandparent about a technology that did not exist when they were young.
- Predict how one technology might change life ten years from now.

## Extensions

- Make a family timeline of technologies used by grandparents, parents, and children.
- Create a healthy habits checklist and check it off for a week.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Aim for balance. Students should be able to say something good and something bad about the same technology.
- "Model" in the standard means the student demonstrates the habits.
