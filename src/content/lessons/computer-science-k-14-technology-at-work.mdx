**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **K.IC.2** The student will discuss and describe how different careers use computing technologies.
  - a. Describe how computing technologies are used in various careers.
  - b. Discuss the advantages of using computing technologies in different careers.
  - c. Identify local jobs that utilize computing technologies.

## Prep

- Collect 5 or 6 pictures of workers.
- Write a short list of three real jobs in your town that use technology, including one held by someone the student knows.
- Optional: arrange for a working adult to answer one question from the student.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "What jobs do you know about? What do those workers do all day?" Then ask, "Do you think any of them use a computer or other technology?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Explain: "A career is the work a person does. Many careers use computing technologies as tools."
- Show three worker pictures and describe one way each uses technology:
  - A nurse or doctor uses a computer to keep notes about patients.
  - A cashier uses a scanner and a screen to add up prices.
  - A delivery driver uses a map on a device to find houses.
- Introduce advantages: "An advantage is a way something makes work better." Give simple ones: faster, fewer mistakes, easier to share information, easier to find things.
- Connect each example to an advantage: "The scanner is faster than typing in every price."
- Bring it home: "People right here in our town use technology at work too." Share one job from your prepared list.

## Guided Practice (5 minutes)

1. Show the remaining worker pictures one at a time. Ask, "How might this worker use technology?" Give hints as needed.
2. For each, ask, "How does the technology help? What is the advantage?"
3. Talk through your local list. For each job, name the workplace the student knows (the grocery store, the library, the fire station) and what technology is used there.
4. Ask, "Who do we know that uses technology at work?"

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (5 minutes)

The student chooses one career, draws the worker using a computing technology, and tells you about the drawing. Then ask:

1. "How does the technology help that worker?"
2. "Tell me one job in our town that uses technology."

Look for:
- The student describes a use of technology that fits the career.
- The student gives at least one advantage.
- The student names at least one local job.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Use two careers the student sees often.
- Act out the job with and without the technology so the advantage is visible.
- Accept pointing and short phrases.

**For students ready for more**
- Compare two careers that use the same technology in different ways.
- Ask what a job might be like if the technology stopped working.

## Extensions

- On the next errand, watch a worker and name the technology they use.
- Ask a family member or neighbor one question: "How do you use technology at your job?"

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- The standard asks for local jobs, so the prepared list matters. Use real workplaces in your community and people the student knows.
- A short conversation with a working adult makes this lesson much stronger than pictures alone.
- Keep advantages concrete: faster, easier, fewer mistakes.
