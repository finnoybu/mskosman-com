**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **6.AP.1** The student will apply computational thinking to identify patterns, make use of decomposition to break down problems or processes into sub-components, and design algorithms.
  - a. Identify patterns and repeated steps in an algorithm, problem, or process.
  - b. Decompose an algorithm, problem, or process into sub-components.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "What is the most complicated thing you have organized or built? How did you keep track of all the parts?"

## Direct Instruction (4 minutes)

- Define sub-component: a part of a problem that can be solved mostly on its own and then combined with the others.
- Model a decomposition tree for the meal plan: budget, menu, shopping list, and schedule, each split again.
- Identify patterns: every day needs the same three decisions, and every meal follows the same steps of choose, check cost, and add to the list.
- Explain the benefit: a repeated pattern becomes one reusable procedure, and separate sub-components can be solved or checked independently.

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. The student builds a decomposition tree for a different problem, such as organizing a small tournament.
2. Mark the repeated steps across branches.
3. Write one repeated pattern as a named procedure.
4. Ask, "Which sub-components depend on others? Which could two people work on at once?"

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

Give a new problem. The student decomposes it to two levels, identifies at least one repeated pattern, and names the procedure that would handle it.

Look for:
- Sub-components are distinct and together cover the problem.
- Repeated steps are identified accurately.
- The student explains how the pattern reduces work.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Decompose to one level and find one pattern.
- Use a problem from the student's own week.

**For students ready for more**
- Identify dependencies between sub-components and order them.
- Find one pattern shared by two unrelated problems.

## Extensions

- Decompose a real upcoming project and assign the parts.
- Look at how a recipe book or manual decomposes a complex task.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- This is the middle school version of work begun in Grade 3. Expect more levels and clearer language.
- Naming a repeated pattern prepares for writing functions.
