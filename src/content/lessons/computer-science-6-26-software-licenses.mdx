**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **6.IC.4** The student will identify copyrighted and licensed software material.
  - a. Identify the role of software licenses, including open-source, and why they are used.
  - b. Compare and contrast the positives and negatives of various software licenses.

## Prep

- Make cards: proprietary (commercial), freeware, subscription, and open source.
- For three programs your family uses, find out which kind of license each has.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "When you buy a game, do you own the game, or something else?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Define software license: the legal agreement that states what a user may and may not do with software. Buying software usually means buying a license, not the software itself.
- Explain why licenses are used: to protect the creators' work, to let them earn from it or share it on their terms, and to tell users their rights.
- Describe the types. Proprietary: the owner keeps the code private and controls use, often for a price. Subscription: pay regularly for access. Freeware: free to use, but the code is not shared. Open source: the code is public and may be used, studied, changed, and shared under stated conditions.
- Model comparing two on cost, support, ability to change it, and updates.

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. The student matches the family's programs to license types.
2. Fill in the comparison table for all four types: positives and negatives.
3. Ask, "Who benefits from each model?"
4. Discuss what copying a paid program for a friend would violate.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student explains what a software license is for, describes open source, and compares two license types with one positive and one negative each.

Look for:
- The role of a license is explained.
- Open source is described accurately.
- The comparison includes both positives and negatives.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Compare two types: proprietary and open source.
- Use a table with headings provided.

**For students ready for more**
- Read the summary of a real open-source license and explain its conditions.
- Find an open-source alternative to a paid program and compare them.

## Extensions

- Check the license of a program before installing it.
- List the software your family pays for and how.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Free in price and open in source are different things.
- This builds on copyright and open source from Grade 5.
