**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **1.CSY.2** The student will use accurate terminology to describe when a computing system might not work as expected.
  - a. Identify and describe a problem with a device or computing system when it does not work as expected.
  - b. Propose a solution to simple hardware or software issues.

## Prep

- Set up two safe problems before the lesson: turn the volume all the way down, and leave one app open on a screen the student will not expect.
- Draw a two-column chart labeled Problem and Try This.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Say, "My tablet is broken." Pause. Ask, "Does that tell you what is wrong?" Then say, "The screen is black and it will not turn on." Ask, "Which one helps more? Why?"

## Direct Instruction (6 minutes)

- Explain: "A detective describes exactly what they see. When a device does not work as expected, we describe the problem with accurate words."
- Teach useful words: screen, sound, volume, battery, charger, frozen, app, loading, connected.
- Teach the difference: "A hardware problem is with a part you can touch, like a charger that is not plugged in. A software problem is with a program, like an app that is frozen."
- Model describing: "The app is open, but there is no sound."
- Fill in the chart with simple problems and solutions:
  - No sound: check the volume.
  - Screen is black: check that it is charged and turned on.
  - App is frozen: close the app and open it again.
  - Mouse or headphones do nothing: check that they are plugged in.
- Add the last row: "If it still does not work, ask a grown-up."

## Guided Practice (5 minutes)

1. Play a sound on the device with the volume down. Ask, "What is the problem? Use detective words."
2. Ask, "Is it a hardware or a software problem? What should we try?" Let the student try the fix.
3. Show the unexpected app screen. Repeat: describe, then propose.
4. Describe a third problem aloud ("The battery picture is red") and have the student propose a solution.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

Give two short problem stories. For each, the student describes the problem in a full sentence and proposes one thing to try.

Look for:
- The description names the part or program and what it is doing.
- The proposed solution fits the problem.
- The student knows to ask a grown-up when the first try does not work.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Provide the sentence frame: "The ___ is ___."
- Use pictures of each problem.
- Practice only the two most common problems in your home.

**For students ready for more**
- Give a problem with two possible causes and ask what to check first.
- The student invents problem stories for you to solve.

## Extensions

- Add to the chart whenever a real problem comes up.
- Use detective words for other things that stop working, such as a toy or a lamp.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Kindergarten taught "stop, look, tell." Grade 1 adds accurate description and a proposed solution.
- Only stage safe problems. Decide ahead of time which fixes the student may try alone and which need you.
- Praise precise words. "The video stopped and there is a spinning circle" is exactly the goal.
