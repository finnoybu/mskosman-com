**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **1.AP.3** The student will use the iterative design process to construct, test, and debug algorithms that include sequencing and an event.
  - a. Discuss and describe the concept of debugging.
  - b. Analyze and explain the results of an algorithm.
  - c. Revise and improve an algorithm to produce desired outcomes.

## Prep

- Write a five-card algorithm with one mistake in it. Example goal: put the stuffed animal in the box. Cards: WHEN the bell rings; pick up the animal; walk to the box; walk back to the start; put the animal down. The mistake is that the animal is put down in the wrong place.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Give the student silly directions for drawing a face: "Draw a circle. Draw two eyes. Draw the mouth above the eyes." Look at the result together. Ask, "What went wrong? Was it you, or was it my directions?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Explain: "A mistake in an algorithm is called a bug. Finding and fixing bugs is called debugging."
- Normalize it: "Everyone who writes algorithms has bugs. Debugging is part of the job."
- Teach three steps and write one word for each on a card:
  1. **Test**: run the algorithm exactly as written.
  2. **Explain**: say what happened and compare it to what we wanted.
  3. **Fix**: change a step and test again.
- Say, "Doing this again and again until it works is how algorithms get better."
- State the goal for your prepared algorithm: the animal should end up in the box.

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. Lay out your five cards. Ring the bell and act out every step exactly, including the mistake.
2. Ask, "What did the algorithm do? What did we want it to do?" Have the student say both.
3. Ask, "Which step has the bug?" Let the student point to it.
4. Fix it together by moving, removing, or rewriting one card.
5. Test again. Ask, "Did it produce what we wanted this time?"

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (5 minutes)

Set a new goal, such as getting the animal onto a chair. The student writes or arranges a short algorithm with an event, tests it with you acting it out, explains the result, and revises it if needed. Then ask, "What is debugging?"

Look for:
- The student describes debugging in their own words.
- The student explains what the algorithm did, not only that it was wrong.
- The student changes a specific step and retests.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Use three cards with one obvious bug, such as two steps in the wrong order.
- Point to each card as it is acted out so the student can see where it goes wrong.
- Offer two choices for the fix.

**For students ready for more**
- Hide two bugs in one algorithm.
- The student writes a buggy algorithm for you to debug.

## Extensions

- Debug real instructions: follow a recipe or building instructions and notice when a step is unclear.
- Keep a "bugs we fixed" list for a week.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Follow the cards literally, even when you can see the mistake coming. The test only teaches if it is honest.
- Step 2 matters most at this grade. Explaining the result is a separate skill from fixing it, and the standard names both.
- Keep the mood light. A bug is a puzzle to solve, not a failure.
