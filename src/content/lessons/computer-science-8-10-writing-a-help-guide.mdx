**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **8.CSY.2** The student will apply computational thinking to troubleshoot and document hardware and software-related problems.
  - a. Apply systematic processes to resolve hardware, software, and connectivity-related problems.
  - b. Design an end-user document/guide to resolve hardware, software, and connectivity-related problems.

## Prep

- Choose three common problems for the guide, one each for hardware, software, and connectivity.
- Arrange for a non-expert to try the finished guide.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "Have you ever followed instructions that skipped a step or used a word you did not know? What happened?"

## Direct Instruction (4 minutes)

- Review the systematic process: define the problem, list causes, order them, test one at a time, confirm, and document.
- Define end user: the person who uses the device, who may have no technical background.
- Teach guide design: a clear title for each problem, symptoms in plain words, numbered steps with one action each, what the user should see after each step, simplest and safest fixes first, pictures or diagrams, and when to stop and get help.
- Explain testing a guide: watch someone follow it without helping them.

## Guided Practice (9 minutes)

1. The student works through one problem systematically and records the steps.
2. Draft the guide section for that problem.
3. Add the other two problems.
4. A tester follows one section while the student observes.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student revises the guide from the tester's experience and delivers the final version.

Look for:
- The troubleshooting process is systematic.
- Steps are numbered, single-action, and in plain language.
- The guide is revised after testing.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Write a guide for one problem.
- Use a template with headings.

**For students ready for more**
- Add a decision tree at the front that directs users to the right section.
- Add screenshots or diagrams.

## Extensions

- Keep the guide with the family's devices.
- Write a guide for a relative's most frequent problem.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Writing for a non-expert is harder than solving the problem. Testing with a real person shows the gaps.
- A good guide says when to stop and ask for help.
