**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **4.CSY.3** The student will describe the learning process of humans and computers.
  - a. Compare and contrast how humans and computing technologies collect, store, and process data.
  - b. Identify similarities and differences on how humans and computing technologies infer and extract meaning from data.
  - c. Define machine learning and identify machine learning approaches: supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning.

## Prep

- Prepare the labeled picture cards.
- Draw a Venn diagram labeled Humans and Computers.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "How did you learn to tell a cat from a dog? Did someone give you a rule, or did you see lots of examples?"

## Direct Instruction (6 minutes)

- Compare in the Venn diagram. Collecting: senses and sensors. Storing: memory in the brain and files in storage. Processing: thinking and following programs.
- Compare finding meaning. Both find patterns in examples. Humans also use experience, context, and feelings and can learn from few examples. Computers need many examples and find only the patterns in the data they are given.
- Define machine learning: "A way for computers to learn patterns from data instead of being given every rule."
- Name the three approaches. Supervised: learning from examples that have labels. Unsupervised: finding groups in data with no labels. Reinforcement: learning by trying actions and getting rewards or penalties.

## Guided Practice (7 minutes)

1. Supervised: show ten labeled cards, then test the student on unlabeled ones.
2. Unsupervised: give unlabeled cards and ask the student to group them by what they notice.
3. Reinforcement: play hot and cold to find a hidden object, giving a token for each move closer.
4. After each, ask, "What data did you learn from?"

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student gives one similarity and one difference in how humans and computers learn, defines machine learning, and matches three short examples to the three approaches.

Look for:
- The comparison covers collecting, storing, or processing.
- Machine learning is defined as learning patterns from data.
- Each approach is matched to the right example.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Teach supervised learning first and the other two in a second session.
- Use the anchors: labels, no labels, rewards.

**For students ready for more**
- Ask what would happen if the labeled examples were wrong.
- Think of a real product that might use each approach.

## Extensions

- Train a family member on a made-up category using labeled examples.
- Notice recommendations in a video or music app and guess what data they learned from.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- The three activities carry the meaning. The vocabulary can settle over time.
- Stay precise: a computer finds patterns in data. It does not understand as a person does.
