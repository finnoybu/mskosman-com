**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **2.CSY.2** The student will demonstrate an understanding of how to troubleshoot simple hardware and software problems that may occur during use.
  - a. Propose solutions to simple hardware and software issues.
  - b. Use appropriate steps to perform simple troubleshooting tasks.

## Prep

- Set up two safe problems: unplug an accessory or the charger, and open an app so that it is hidden behind another window or screen.
- Decide which fixes the student may do alone.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "What do you do when a flashlight will not turn on?" List the student's ideas in the order they give them. Point out that they are checking things one at a time.

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Explain: "Troubleshooting means finding out what is wrong, one step at a time, and trying a fix."
- Write a checklist together: 1. Stop and look. What exactly is happening? 2. Check the simple things: power, cables, volume, connection. 3. Close the app and open it again. 4. Restart the device, with permission. 5. Ask for help and say what you already tried.
- Explain why order matters: start with the fastest and safest checks.
- Review the two kinds of problems: hardware (parts) and software (programs).

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. Present the first problem. The student works down the checklist, saying each step aloud.
2. Ask, "Was this a hardware or a software problem? What solved it?"
3. Present the second problem and repeat.
4. Describe a third problem in words and have the student propose which steps to try.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

Describe two new problems: "The screen is frozen" and "The printer does nothing." For each, the student proposes a solution and tells you the troubleshooting steps in order.

Look for:
- The student follows the steps in order.
- The proposed solution fits the problem.
- The student reports what was tried when asking for help.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Put a picture beside each checklist step.
- Practice with one type of problem until the steps feel routine.

**For students ready for more**
- Give a problem with two possible causes and ask how to tell which it is.
- Have the student add a step to the checklist for a problem your family often has.

## Extensions

- Post the checklist near the family computer.
- Keep a log of real problems and what fixed them.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Grade 1 asked students to describe a problem and suggest a fix. Grade 2 adds a repeatable sequence of steps.
- Restarting and anything involving settings should stay with you unless you have agreed otherwise.
