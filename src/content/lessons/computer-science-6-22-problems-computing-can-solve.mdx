**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **6.IC.1** The student will assess the impact of computing technologies on local society.
  - c. Research simple and complex problems that computing systems can be used to solve.
  - d. Analyze the implications of emerging technologies and potential real-world impact in the local community.

## Prep

- Bookmark two short articles: one about computing applied to a problem, such as weather forecasting, traffic, or disease tracking, and one about an emerging technology relevant to your area, such as drones, self-checkout, telehealth, or AI tools.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "What is a problem in our town that a computer could help with? What is one a computer could not solve alone?"

## Direct Instruction (4 minutes)

- Distinguish simple problems, with clear rules and few variables such as sorting records, from complex problems, with many interacting factors and uncertainty such as predicting weather.
- Explain how computing helps: storing and searching large amounts of data, calculating quickly, modeling, automating, and connecting people.
- Note limits: computing cannot decide what is fair or valuable, depends on good data, and can create new problems.
- Define implications: the likely effects, intended and unintended, on jobs, services, privacy, cost, and access.

## Guided Practice (8 minutes)

1. The student reads the first article and fills in the note sheet.
2. Classify the problem as simple or complex and explain.
3. Read the second article. List possible effects on the local community.
4. Sort those effects into benefits and concerns, and note who is affected.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student presents one problem computing helps solve and its limits, and an analysis of one emerging technology's likely impact on the local community.

Look for:
- Simple and complex problems are distinguished.
- Research is summarized in the student's own words.
- The analysis includes benefits, concerns, and who is affected.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Research one article with guided notes.
- List two effects.

**For students ready for more**
- Propose how computing could help with a specific local problem and what data it would need.
- Find a case where a technology had an unexpected local effect.

## Extensions

- Attend or watch a local government meeting where a technology decision is discussed.
- Write a letter stating a view on a local technology issue.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Encourage specific local thinking: this town, these people.
- Unintended effects are worth dwelling on.
