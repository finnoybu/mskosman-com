**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **2.IC.2** The student will explain the need to balance screen time and other activities.
  - a. Discuss appropriate times and places for screen use.
  - b. List and describe alternatives to screen time.

## Prep

- Write twelve cards, such as at the dinner table, in the car on a long trip, right before bed, during a lesson, at a friend's house, in the waiting room, while walking, at a restaurant, on a rainy afternoon, in the bathroom, during a family movie night, when a friend is visiting.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "When was the last time a screen got in the way of something else you wanted or needed to do?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Explain balance: "Balance means having enough of many different things: moving, sleeping, playing, learning, time with people, and some screen time."
- Discuss time: screens are a poor fit right before sleep, during meals, and when someone is talking to you.
- Discuss place: screens do not belong where they are unsafe or rude, such as while walking near a street.
- Sort two cards aloud into okay, not okay, and it depends, giving a reason.
- Introduce alternatives: "An alternative is something else you could do instead." Name a few and describe what makes each enjoyable.

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. The student sorts the remaining cards and explains each choice.
2. Talk through the "it depends" cards. What does it depend on?
3. Divide the paper plate into sections like a pie. The student draws a balanced day with screen time as one slice.
4. Together, list eight alternatives to screen time and describe what each one involves.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student tells you two good times or places for screens and two poor ones, with reasons. Then the student lists five alternatives and describes one in detail.

Look for:
- The student gives reasons tied to safety, sleep, manners, or attention.
- The alternatives are real activities the student would do.
- The student describes an alternative, not only names it.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Use six cards with clear answers.
- Keep a picture menu of alternatives where the student can see it.

**For students ready for more**
- Design a one-week plan with a different alternative each day.
- Discuss why it can be hard to stop using a screen.

## Extensions

- Make an "instead of a screen" jar with activity ideas on slips of paper.
- Try a family screen-free hour and talk about how it went.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Your family's limits are your decision. This lesson gives the student language to discuss them.
- Avoid making screens the villain. The standard is about balance.
