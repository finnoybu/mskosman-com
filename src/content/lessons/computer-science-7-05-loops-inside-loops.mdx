**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **7.AP.2** The student will plan and implement algorithms that include sequencing, loops, variables, user input, conditional control structures, and functions using a block-based or text-based programming tool.
  - d. Read and write programs that contain nested conditionals and nested loops.

## Prep

- Write a sample with a nested loop that draws or prints a grid, such as 4 rows of 6 symbols.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "On a clock, how many times does the minute hand go around for one trip of the hour hand? Which one is the inner loop?"

## Direct Instruction (4 minutes)

- Define nested loop: a loop inside another. The inner loop runs completely for each single pass of the outer loop.
- Trace the sample on grid paper: the outer loop is the rows and the inner loop is the columns. Count the total repetitions by multiplying.
- Review nested conditionals and show one inside a loop.
- Explain reading nested code: use the indentation or block shape, and trace from the innermost part outward.

## Guided Practice (8 minutes)

1. The student predicts the sample's output, then runs it.
2. Change the loop counts and predict again.
3. The student writes a nested loop that makes a different pattern, such as a triangle or a multiplication table.
4. Add a nested conditional that changes what is produced in certain positions.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (5 minutes)

The student writes a program with a nested loop and a nested conditional, and states before running how many times the innermost step will execute.

Look for:
- The inner loop completes for each outer pass.
- The repetition count is predicted correctly.
- Nesting is correctly structured.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Trace on grid paper before writing code.
- Start with a 2 by 3 grid.

**For students ready for more**
- Use the loop variables in a calculation.
- Nest three levels and explain the count.

## Extensions

- Print a multiplication table.
- Generate a patterned design.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- The total number of inner repetitions is the product of the loop counts. That makes nested loops powerful and sometimes slow.
- Tracing on paper prevents most confusion.
