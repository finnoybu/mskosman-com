**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **4.CSY.1** The student will model how a computing system works to accomplish a task.
  - a. Describe how computing systems perceive the world through sensors and other inputs.
  - b. Compare and contrast how humans and computers process information from inputs.
  - c. Explain how computing devices may be used to classify and organize input.

## Prep

- Draw the chart with rows for sight, hearing, touch, and other.
- If the device's photo app groups pictures or recognizes faces or objects, be ready to show it.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask the student to close their eyes and identify three sounds in the room. Ask, "What did your ears collect? What did your brain do with it?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Explain perceive: to take in information about the world. Humans use senses. Computers use sensors and other inputs.
- Fill in the chart: eyes and camera, ears and microphone, skin and touchscreen or temperature sensor. Add sensors with no human match, such as GPS.
- Compare processing. Alike: both take in input, process it, and respond. Different: a human uses experience, feelings, and context, while a computer follows instructions and works with numbers very fast without tiring.
- Explain classifying: a computer can sort input into groups by its features, such as photos by date or by what is in them, and organize it so it can be found.

## Guided Practice (6 minutes)

1. The student adds two more rows to the chart.
2. Ask, "What can you tell from a photo that a computer might miss? What can a computer do with a thousand photos that you could not?"
3. The student classifies the picture cards into groups and states the rule used.
4. Show how the device organizes photos, or describe it, and compare with the student's groups.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student describes two sensors and what each perceives, gives one similarity and one difference between human and computer processing, and explains one way a computer classifies input.

Look for:
- Sensors are linked to what they detect.
- The comparison includes both a similarity and a difference.
- Classification is explained as grouping by features.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Complete the chart for sight and hearing only.
- Sort six picture cards.

**For students ready for more**
- Find a sensor that detects something humans cannot sense.
- Discuss when a computer's classification might be wrong.

## Extensions

- List every sensor you can find in a phone or a car.
- Sort a photo collection two ways and compare with how the device does it.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Avoid saying a computer "sees" or "understands" as a person does. "Detects" and "processes" are more accurate.
- This lesson prepares for the machine learning ideas in Lesson 11.
