**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **3.NI.1** The student will describe computing networks.
  - a. Differentiate between a network and the Internet.
  - b. Identify the components of a computing network.
  - c. Describe how a computing device connects to a network.
  - d. Identify ways networks are used to transmit information.

## Prep

- Make the component cards.
- Find where your router is so you can show it.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "How does a page get from the computer to the printer in the other room when there is no cable between them?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Define network: "Two or more computing devices connected so they can share information." Your home devices form a network.
- Define the Internet: "A worldwide network made of millions of smaller networks connected together." A home network is one small piece.
- Name the components: devices such as computers and phones, a router that directs information between them, connections that are either cables or wireless signals (Wi-Fi), and servers, which are computers that store and send out information.
- Describe connecting: a device joins by cable or by choosing the Wi-Fi network and giving its password. The router then lets it send and receive.
- List ways information travels: through cables, through radio signals such as Wi-Fi and cell signals, and through fiber-optic lines that carry light.

## Guided Practice (7 minutes)

1. Tape the cards to a table or wall and connect them with yarn to build a home network, with the router in the center.
2. Add a line from the router going off the edge, labeled "to the Internet."
3. The student traces the path of a message from the tablet to the printer, and from the laptop to a website.
4. Visit the real router. Find its cables and lights.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student explains the difference between a network and the Internet, names three network components, and describes how a tablet connects to the home network.

Look for:
- A network is described as connected devices and the Internet as networks joined worldwide.
- Components named include the router.
- The student identifies at least two ways information is transmitted.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Build the yarn model with three devices and a router.
- Use the phrase "the Internet is a network of networks" as an anchor.

**For students ready for more**
- Add a second home network and connect the two routers to show how the Internet grows.
- Find out what the lights on your router mean.

## Extensions

- Draw a map of the devices on your home network.
- Notice where Wi-Fi is strong and weak in your home and guess why.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- The yarn model is the centerpiece. Let the student build and narrate it.
- Do not share the real Wi-Fi password as part of the lesson. Connecting can be described in words.
