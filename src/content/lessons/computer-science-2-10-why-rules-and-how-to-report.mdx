**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **2.CYB.1** The student will model safe and responsible behaviors when using information and computing technologies.
  - a. Explain the need for safe and responsible uses of computing technologies.
  - b. Create a flowchart to illustrate the process for reporting inappropriate use of technology at school or at home.

## Prep

- Review your technology rules and your reporting steps so they are current.
- Draw one example flowchart for something simple, such as crossing a street.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "What could go wrong if people used technology with no rules at all?" List three ideas.

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Explain the need: rules protect people's safety, their private information, their feelings, and the devices themselves.
- Connect each of your rules to one of those four reasons.
- Introduce flowcharts: "A flowchart is a drawing of steps with arrows that show what to do next." Show your example. Point out the start, the boxes for steps, the diamond for a question, and the arrows.
- Review the reporting steps: stop, leave the screen as it is, tell a trusted grown-up.
- Model starting a reporting flowchart: Start, then a diamond asking, "Did I see or get something that is not okay?"

## Guided Practice (7 minutes)

1. Write each reporting step on a sticky note together.
2. The student arranges the notes in order on the paper and draws arrows.
3. Add the question diamond with a yes arrow and a no arrow.
4. Add a box for who to tell at home and a box for who to tell at school or another place.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student redraws the flowchart neatly on their own and uses it to walk you through one situation. Then ask, "Why do we need safe and responsible use of technology?"

Look for:
- The flowchart has a start, steps in order, arrows, and at least one decision.
- It names trusted adults for home and for other places.
- The student gives a real reason for the rules.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Provide the shapes already drawn and let the student fill them in.
- Make a three-box flowchart with no decision.

**For students ready for more**
- Add a second decision, such as "Is a trusted grown-up nearby?"
- Make a flowchart for another rule, such as what to do when screen time ends.

## Extensions

- Post the flowchart near the family computer.
- Make a flowchart for a non-technology routine to practice the shapes.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- The standard says "at school or at home," so include both branches even if the student learns at home.
- A flowchart is also an algorithm with a decision in it. Point that out.
