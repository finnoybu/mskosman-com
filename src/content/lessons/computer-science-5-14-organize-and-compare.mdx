**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **5.DA.1** The student will collect data or use data sets to solve a problem or investigate a topic.
  - c. Organize data based on similarities or patterns.
  - d. Compare and contrast various data elements.

## Prep

- Make the data cards from a reference book or a site you trust.
- Decide whether to work on paper or in a spreadsheet.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Spread out the cards in no order. Ask, "What question could this answer? What makes it hard right now?"

## Direct Instruction (4 minutes)

- Explain organizing: group items that are alike, or order them by a value, so patterns show.
- Model grouping by one attribute and ordering by another.
- Define data element: one piece of data about one item, such as one animal's weight.
- Model comparing and contrasting: choose two items and say what is the same and different across their elements. Then compare two groups.

## Guided Practice (7 minutes)

1. The student groups the cards by one attribute and records the groups.
2. Reorder the same cards by a numeric attribute.
3. Ask, "What pattern shows up now that did not before?"
4. The student compares two items in full sentences using at least three elements.

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

The student organizes the data a third way of their choice and writes a short comparison of two groups, with one similarity and two differences.

Look for:
- The organization is consistent and has a stated basis.
- A pattern is identified from it.
- The comparison uses specific data elements.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Use eight cards and two attributes.
- Use a two-circle Venn diagram to compare.

**For students ready for more**
- Organize by two attributes at once in a table.
- Find an item that does not fit the pattern and suggest why.

## Extensions

- Organize a personal collection in a spreadsheet.
- Compare two cities, teams, or planets using data.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Organizing data in different ways answers different questions. Have the student try at least two.
- Keep the cards for Lesson 15 if the student wants to continue with them.
