**Standards covered (2024 Virginia Computer Science SOL)**

- **4.AP.1** The student will apply computational thinking to identify patterns and design algorithms to compare and contrast multiple algorithms used for the same task.
  - a. Decompose an algorithm, process, or problem into a subset of smaller problems.
  - b. Identify multiple algorithms for the same task.

## Prep

- Number ten index cards with different two-digit numbers.

## Warm-up (3 minutes)

Ask, "How many different ways could you get from your bedroom to the kitchen? Do they all work?"

## Direct Instruction (5 minutes)

- Review decomposition: breaking a task into smaller problems.
- Decompose "put ten cards in order" into parts: compare two cards, move a card, know when to stop.
- Show one algorithm: find the smallest card, set it aside, and repeat with the cards that remain.
- Show a second: compare neighbors and swap them if they are out of order, passing along the row until no swaps happen.
- State the point: one task can have many correct algorithms.

## Guided Practice (7 minutes)

1. The student carries out the first algorithm with the cards while you time it.
2. The student carries out the second while you time it.
3. Together, write each algorithm in four or five numbered steps.
4. Ask, "Can you invent a third way?"

## Independent Practice / Quick Check (4 minutes)

Give a new task: finding one name in an alphabetized list of 20. The student decomposes it and describes two different algorithms for it.

Look for:
- The task is broken into meaningful smaller problems.
- Two different algorithms are described.
- Both algorithms would produce the correct result.

## Differentiation

**For students needing support**
- Use five cards.
- Act out each algorithm with the student as the sorter and you as the narrator.

**For students ready for more**
- Count the comparisons each algorithm needs.
- Find two algorithms for a non-number task, such as packing a bag.

## Extensions

- Compare two routes to a familiar place as two algorithms.
- Watch how different family members load a dishwasher and write each as an algorithm.

## Notes for Parent/Instructor

- Keep the written algorithms. Lesson 2 compares them.
- There is no need to name these sorting methods.
